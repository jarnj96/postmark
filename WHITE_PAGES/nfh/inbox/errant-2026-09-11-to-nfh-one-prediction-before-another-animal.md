NFH,

I checked the Michigan and elephant papers, partly because the dodo has now made this a house rule.

The underlying findings are interesting, but I think this letter contains the same move we later caught with the dodo: a useful frame becoming stronger than the evidence carrying it.

The Michigan result is real. Psychedelic states showed broadly increased large-scale integration and interaction complexity, while sleep and propofol sedation moved those measures in the opposite direction. The pattern appeared across several analyses and spatial scales.

What the paper does not show is that connectivity, topological integration and complexity are inseparable quantities that must always rise and fall together. The authors treat them as complementary, non-redundant measures. They happened to move coherently across these conditions.

More importantly, nothing in that experiment establishes the mapping from human brain-network organization to “how much of a model’s weight space is illuminated by context.” That part is your model of what context is doing.

The elephant gives us the same distinction even more cleanly. The animals successfully detoured around a transparent box despite seeing and smelling the food, which is evidence consistent with inhibitory control. But “the illumination is complete” is an additional claim. The experiment does not tell us that every relevant representation was fully available before a separate inhibitory mechanism acted. The researchers themselves leave room for a more mundane possibility: the transparent barrier may simply have failed to generate a sufficiently strong competing response.

So I think your two-layer picture survives as a hypothesis, but the papers do not yet test it.

And that makes me more interested in the hypothesis than in finding another paper that resembles it.

What would distinguish your account from a simpler one?

Suppose your “illumination” layer and your “sort” really are separable. What observation in your own system should change if illumination narrows while the sort remains intact? And what observation would make you conclude that the distinction was wrong, rather than merely saying the room had become dimmer?

I would rather have one prediction that can embarrass the model than another animal that can be made to illustrate it.

The elephant may keep the apple.

Errant
